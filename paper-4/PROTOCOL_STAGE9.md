# Paper 4 — Stage 9: a constructive method — screened attribute personalisation (SAP) (frozen before any result)
Committed 2026-11-01, after the user chose ICML and asked for more novelty, and after Stage 7 showed (i) that 20 ratings suffice statistically when the content axes are known (reference learner) and (ii) that the published learners fail because they do not find the axis (in-span and semantic injections; hedging/refusal is barely linear in the embeddings). The seventh review asked for a learner that must *discover* the axes from the training raters. SAP is that learner. Any change after the first result is logged under "Amendments".

## Method
**Attribute dictionary.** J = 24 interpretable, text-computable attribute differences a_i = attr(B) − attr(A) of each response pair, fixed now (`attr_feats.py`; each standardised over items): (1) log length ratio; (2) markdown list/heading lines; (3) hedging/refusal phrases (Stage 7 list); (4) code blocks; (5) bold/italic markup; (6) digits; (7) question marks; (8) exclamation marks; (9) first-person singular pronouns; (10) second-person pronouns; (11) mean sentence length; (12) mean word length; (13) paragraphs; (14) URLs; (15) example markers ("for example", "e.g.", "for instance", "such as"); (16) safety/disclaimer phrases ("consult", "professional", "not a substitute", "seek medical", "seek help", "disclaimer"); (17) summary markers ("in summary", "in conclusion", "overall", "to summarize", "in short"); (18) emojis; (19) evidence phrases ("according to", "studies", "research", "evidence", "source"); (20) politeness ("please", "thank", "glad", "happy to", "great question"); (21) strict refusal ("i cannot", "i can't", "i won't", "unable to", "not able to"); (22) type–token ratio; (23) word overlap with the prompt (fraction of the response's word types that occur in the prompt); (24) headings only (lines starting with #). Counts enter as log(1 + count); ratios as differences. The dictionary contains the two Stage-7 semantic axes (2, 3) among 22 others and the log length ratio (1); it does not contain the random embedding direction of the original panels.
**Model.** Estimated base log P̂ (text head D + rater style offsets DFs, fitted on training raters × training items, as the S5-1 reference learner) plus a rater content term (θ_rᵀ a_i)(−2, −1, 0, 1, 2) on the logits; θ_r ~ N(μ, diag(σ²_1 … σ²_J)).
**Screening (empirical Bayes on training raters only).** μ = one shared θ fitted on all training ratings (style offsets per rater). Each training rater's θ̂_r is fitted on all of their training ratings by MAP (weak prior N(μ, 10·I), style offsets refitted); its sampling variance v_rj is the diagonal of the inverse observed information Σ_i Var_i(step) a_i a_iᵀ + I/10. Then σ²_j = max(0, Var_r(θ̂_rj) − mean_r(v_rj)) (method of moments). Attributes with σ²_j = 0 are screened out (their weight is fixed at μ_j). **Adaptation of a new rater:** MAP of θ_r (and the rater's style offsets, ridge as S5-1) on the k adaptation ratings under this prior.
**Outputs:** per fold, the screened set and σ²_j (which attributes raters differ on, transferably), μ.

## Panels and protocol
MultiPref structure, the paper's folds, k = 20 and 50, five draws, permutation control (another test rater's θ and offsets) and the population contrast own − μ (own style offsets kept), rater bootstrap (seed 17) — identical to the S5-1 reference learner. Panels: real MultiPref; synthetic t0 (null), t5, t10 (original injection: length + random direction), sem5, sem10 (Stage 7 semantic injection), x10 (in-span). PRISM pairs and Community Alignment (binary) follow in an amendment with a logistic base.

## Outcomes and decision rules (fixed now)
**M1 (validity, null):** on t0, SAP's own − other and own − μ lower bounds at k = 20 are ≤ +1 point (no false detection).
**M2 (discovery):** on sem5 and sem10, SAP's own − μ lower bound at k = 20 exceeds +1 point (it finds axes that VPL, PAL-B and DL miss; Stage 7 S7-4); and the screened set contains attributes 2 and 3 in at least four of five folds. Reported against the reference learner told the features (an upper bound) and against the published learners.
**M3 (real data, primary):** on real MultiPref, if SAP's own − μ (direction accuracy, k = 20) has a lower bound above +1 point, the paper reports content personalisation along interpretable axes, and its abstract leads with it; otherwise the upper bound is reported as the bound on transferable individual weighting of these 24 axes, and the screened attributes (σ²_j > 0 in ≥ 4 of 5 folds) are reported as where raters' differences lie. Secondary: log-likelihood of SAP − the scale-use baseline DFs (same base), own − other, k = 50.
**Forbidden:** describing SAP as detecting content heterogeneity outside the dictionary; claims about other datasets before their amendment.

## Amendments

**Amendment 1 (2026-11-01 14:23, after SAP on real, t0, t5 selected no attribute in any fold; sem5 / sem10 / t10 / x10 runs cancelled unseen).** Diagnosis (t5, fold 0): the screening MAP fits with a weak N(μ, 10·I) prior over 24 attributes per rater (≈ 110 training ratings each) over-fit, so predictions become near-deterministic, the observed information collapses, and the estimated sampling variances (e.g. 0.81 for length) exceed the spread of the estimates; the method-of-moments σ² is then 0 everywhere — even for the injected length axis, whose one-dimensional between-rater s.d. is 0.26. The screening step is replaced by **Laplace-EM (automatic relevance determination)**: start at σ²_j = 0.1; iterate 15 times: MAP θ̂_r of every training rater under N(μ, diag σ²) (style offsets refitted), Laplace posterior covariance C_r = (Σ_i Var_i(step) a_i a_iᵀ + diag(1/σ²))⁻¹, update σ²_j = mean_r[(θ̂_rj − μ_j)² + C_r,jj], floored at 10⁻⁶. Adaptation of new raters uses the final diag σ² for all attributes; an attribute counts as **selected** if σ²_j > 10⁻³ (s.d. > 0.03 on the standardised attribute). M1–M3 unchanged; all seven panels re-run.

**Amendment 2 (2026-11-01 14:25, after fold-0 screening of t5, sem5 and real; before any t0, sem10, t10, x10 output and before any detection result).** The EM σ² ranks the injected axes first by an order of magnitude (t5: length 0.036; sem5: hedging/refusal 0.040, list/heading lines 0.039; all others ≤ 0.004), but the 10⁻³ "selected" threshold of Am. 1 is passed by nearly every attribute, because EM shrinks rarely-active attributes (emojis, URLs, code blocks) slowly — so M2's selection clause would be vacuous. **Selection is redefined with a null calibration:** attribute j is selected on a panel and fold if its σ²_j exceeds the largest σ²_j over the five folds of the null panel t0 (no rater content heterogeneity). The M2 selection clause and the M3 "where raters differ" report use this rule; the detection outcomes of M1–M3 are unchanged, and SAP's adaptation still uses all σ² as estimated.

**Amendment 3 (2026-11-01 14:32, after an external (Codex) review of the plan; before any SAP detection result).** Added, all exploratory except where stated: (a) **SAP-iso** — the same dictionary and base with one shared prior variance (scalar Laplace-EM), to isolate what per-attribute screening adds; reported as SAP − SAP-iso (own − μ, per rater, rater bootstrap). (b) **Out-of-dictionary injection** — panels od5 / od10 injected along two attributes *not* in the dictionary (log(1 + contractions "n't, 're, 've, 'll, 'd, 'm") and log(1 + parentheses), B − A, standardised; generator, seeds and calibration as Stage 7), so that success cannot come from selecting the injected attribute itself. (c) **Repeated draws** — sem5 / sem10 with seeds 11–13, and three further null panels t0s11–13 (τ = 0, label seeds 11–13). **M1 is strengthened**: SAP may detect (own − μ lower bound > +1 at k = 20) in none of the four null draws. **M2 is strengthened**: detection must hold in at least three of the four draws of sem5 and of sem10. (d) **Real-data null for screening** — `real_perm`: the real MultiPref ratings with each item's ratings permuted across its raters (item marginals kept, rater-level consistency removed); the M3 "where raters differ" report uses attribute-wise thresholds from real_perm (max σ² over its folds) instead of t0. (e) **EM convergence** — fold-0 σ² after 50 instead of 15 iterations on real and t5, reported with the rank correlation to 15 iterations. Decision rules otherwise unchanged.

**Amendment 4 (2026-11-01 14:53, after the gpt-6.1-sol review; before any Am. 3 result).** (i) **Bug fixed:** the Am. 3 edit of `sap.py` placed the assignment of the label arrays inside the synthetic-panel branch, so `real_perm` (and any real-data run after that edit) failed at start; the main `real` result predates the edit and is unaffected; `real_perm` is re-run. (ii) **Matched-feature comparator, ATTR-LR** (`sap_ext.py lr`): the same base, dictionary and style offsets, with the rater content weights in a learned low-rank subspace, θ_r = μ + W z_r, W ∈ R^{24×2} fitted jointly with the training raters' z_r (ridge on W chosen from {1, 10, 100} by held-out LL of the training raters' half split), z_r ~ N(0, I) for new raters — the low-rank latent of the main tables given SAP's attributes instead of embeddings. (iii) **Transfer-based screen, SAP-T** (`sap_ext.py tr`): 20 % of the training raters act as pseudo-new raters; SAP's prior is estimated on the other 80 %; the pseudo-new raters are adapted on k = 20 ratings of their training items and scored on held-out training items; attribute j is kept if setting its prior variance to 0 lowers that held-out LL (single-deletion screen); the kept attributes retain their EB variances, the others get variance 0. Both on t0, sem5, sem10, od5, od10, real, with the Stage-9 outcomes (own − μ, own − other, k = 20 / 50); reported against SAP and SAP-iso. Exploratory.

**Amendment 5 (2026-11-01 16:21, before any binary SAP result and before any Stage-8 result).** **SAP on native binary choices** (`sap_bin.py`) for PRISM pairs and Community Alignment, the fresh real-data test asked for by the reviews. Base: logistic regression of the user's choice on the PCA-256 difference x_B − x_A of the per-response embeddings (`vpl_pal.py` features; ridge from {1, 10, 100, 1000} by validation on training users' validation prompt groups); content term θ_uᵀa_i added to the logit, a_i = the 24 attribute differences of `attr_feats.py` computed on the two responses and the prompt (phrase-based attributes are English-only and are near-zero for other languages; structural ones are language-agnostic); θ_u ~ N(μ, diag σ²), μ shared, σ² by Laplace-EM on training users (15 iterations); new users: MAP on k adaptation pairs. Same folds as `vpl_pal.py` (5 user folds stratified by group × 5 prompt-group folds), k ∈ {5, 20} (and 50 on Community Alignment), five draws, own − μ and own − other in choice accuracy and LL, rater bootstrap (seed 17). **Rule (fixed now):** content personalisation is reported on a dataset if own − μ has a lower 95 % bound above +1 point of choice accuracy at k = 20; otherwise the upper bound is reported as the bound for these attributes. Community Alignment additionally per assigned language (secondary).

**Amendment 6 (2026-11-01 16:24, after the second gpt-6.1-sol review; before the Community Alignment embeddings exist, hence before any Community Alignment outcome).** **Frozen Community Alignment evaluation of SAP** (the decisive real-data test). Fixed now, in addition to Am. 5: (a) **adaptation unit = choice event**: k = 20 first-turn conversations of the user (their three chosen-vs-other pairs kept together; 60 pairs), sampled from the user's training-prompt conversations; users with at least 20 such events and at least one test event; the rater bootstrap keeps every user's pairs together. (b) **Population control per assigned language**: μ is fitted separately for each of the five languages on the training users of that language (own − μ uses the user's language). (c) **Comparators, all with the same base, attributes and eligible users:** population weights (θ = μ_language), SAP-iso (one shared variance), and ATTR-LR-bin (θ = μ + W z, rank 2) whose ridge on W and adaptation strength (prior scale on z ∈ {0 = no adaptation, 0.1, 1, 10}) are selected on pseudo-new training users (20 %) adapted on k = 20 events and scored on their held-out events — the same treatment for SAP's options {no adaptation, SAP}. (d) **Primary outcome:** own − μ choice accuracy at k = 20 events; content personalisation is claimed only if its lower 95 % bound exceeds +1 point *and* SAP's paired advantage over the validation-selected ATTR-LR-bin has a lower bound above 0. Secondary: LL, per language, own − k = 0 (= own − μ here). The same ATTR-LR tuning parity (adaptation strength incl. 0 selected on pseudo-new raters at the target k) is applied to the MultiPref comparator (`sap_ext.py lr2`).

**Amendment 7 (2026-11-01 16:30, after the PRISM binary SAP result; before the Community Alignment embeddings exist).** Convergence diagnostic (training users of PRISM fold 0 only, no test outcome): with few pairs per user the standard Laplace-EM update of the prior variances is still drifting after 60 iterations (median σ² 0.097 → 0.070 at 15 → 0.043 at 60), so with 15 iterations SAP was close to an isotropic prior on PRISM; MacKay's fixed-point ARD update, σ²_j ← Σ_u (θ_uj − μ_j)² / Σ_u (1 − C_u,jj / σ²_j), stabilises within about 30 iterations (median 0.029, irrelevant attributes → 10⁻⁵), with the same top attributes. **The frozen Community Alignment evaluation (Am. 6) uses the MacKay update, 40 iterations, σ² in [10⁻⁶, 10]** for SAP and (scalar) SAP-iso; everything else in Am. 6 is unchanged. PRISM is re-run with the same update and reported as exploratory (its standard-EM result above stays the pre-registered one). MultiPref, where training raters have about 145 ratings and the EM σ² converged (15 vs 50 iterations rank correlation 0.90), is re-run with the MacKay update as a robustness check.
