# Paper 4 — Stage 6: LoRe under the new-rater protocol (frozen before any result)
Committed 2026-10-31, after five ICML-style reviews asked for LoRe (Bose et al., 2025) and for a reconciliation with its reported PRISM result: unseen-user binary accuracy 71.0 for LoRe vs 61.0 for a monolithic Bradley–Terry model (VPL 58.2, PAL 59.0), with about four few-shot examples per user and a by-interaction split. That gain contradicts our PRISM ranking null; this stage tests it under our protocol. Any change after the first result is logged under "Amendments".

## Method (as in the LoRe paper, details it leaves open fixed here)
Reward basis R(x, y) = A·e(x, y), a linear map of frozen per-response embeddings to B rewards (we use the full 3,584-d Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct last-token states, standardised; LoRe used Skywork-Reward embeddings — a difference we state); user weights w_u on the simplex (softmax of free logits); score for a pair = w_uᵀ(R(x, y_B) − R(x, y_A)); binary labels: logistic loss (LoRe's); graded labels: ordered-logistic link on the same score (as for VPL / PAL here). Joint training of A and the training users' weights with Adam (learning rate chosen from {0.5 (LoRe's), 0.05, 0.005} by validation log-likelihood), early stopping on validation, B chosen from {2, 5, 10, 20, 50} by validation log-likelihood (LoRe: cross-validated over the same grid). **New users:** A frozen; w_u initialised at the mean of the training users' weight logits and optimised on the user's k adaptation pairs with Adam, learning rate 0.1 (LoRe's), 100 steps, no regularisation (LoRe's). k = 0: the training users' mean weights.

## Panels and protocol
PRISM pairs (native binary choice, E2; and the graded score-difference label, E1) and MultiPref (E1, E2), the paper's folds, k-shot draws and permutation control; k ∈ {0, 5, 20} on PRISM pairs (LoRe used ≈ 4), {0, 20, 50} on MultiPref. Reported alongside the text-only model (Dp) on the same features.

## Outcomes and decision rule (fixed now)
Primary (PRISM pairs, E2): LoRe's own gain in choice accuracy, LoRe(k) − LoRe(0), and own − other, at k = 5 and k = 20, rater bootstrap; and LoRe(k) − Dp. **If the lower 95 % bound of LoRe's own gain or own − other at k = 20 exceeds +1 point, the paper's PRISM ranking null is withdrawn** and the abstract, §6 and conclusions are rewritten around it. Otherwise the paper reports LoRe as a further method with no ranking gain under the new-rater protocol, and discusses the difference from the published +10 points (embeddings, split, number of examples, baseline strength). Secondary: the same on MultiPref, graded labels, and log-likelihood.

## Amendments

**Amendment 1 (2026-10-31 21:00, after the smoke test and two of five PRISM-pairs folds of the faithful LoRe — its unregularised few-shot fit lowers choice accuracy by about 5 points at k = 5 — and before any other Stage-6 result).** To give LoRe its best chance, a **regularised variant (LoRe-reg)** is added, as was done for PAL-B: the same trained basis, the same few-shot optimiser, plus an L2 penalty λ‖w_u − w̄‖² on the user's weight logits towards the training mean, λ ∈ {0, 0.1, 1, 10, 100} chosen per k by k-shot validation on 20 % held-out training raters (LoRe refitted without them with its selected B and learning rate). The decision rule of this protocol applies to **both** variants: the PRISM ranking null is withdrawn if either has a lower 95 % bound above +1 point (own gain or own − other, k = 20, PRISM pairs, binary). Runs: tag `_lorer`, same panels, folds, k and permutation control.

**Amendment 2 (2026-11-01 01:30, after the sixth review asked to reproduce LoRe's published PRISM number in its native setting and localise the gap; before any run below).** Two factors are changed one at a time, with the same LoRe code:
(F1) **Embeddings:** Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B-v0.2 (LoRe cites "Skywork-reward, Liu et al., 2024" without a version; v0.2 is used and the choice is stated) — last-layer hidden state at the final token of the chat-formatted (user prompt, response), 4,096-d, for every PRISM utterance; also its scalar reward.
(F2) **Split and pair construction (LoRe-native):** users with at least six dialogues; users split at random 50/50 into seen and unseen; for every user, half of their interactions (at random) are training / few-shot and half are test; **one pair per interaction** (the chosen response against one randomly drawn other response; ≈ 4 few-shot pairs per unseen user, as LoRe reports); LoRe (B and learning rate by validation on seen users' held-out pairs) and a monolithic Bradley–Terry model (the same linear map with B = 1) are trained on seen users' training pairs; unseen users are adapted on their few-shot pairs and evaluated on their test pairs; 5 random user splits (LoRe used 20).
Runs: (a) native split + Skywork; (b) native split + Qwen features; (c) our protocol (Stage 6 main) + Skywork. Reported: unseen-user binary accuracy of LoRe, BT and LoRe(k = 0), and LoRe − BT, per run. Reading: if (a) reproduces a gain of several points over BT and (c) does not, the gap is attributed to the split/pair construction; if (a) does not reproduce it, we report that LoRe's published number is not reproduced in its native setting with our implementation. No change to the paper's decision rules.

**Amendment 3 (2026-11-01 01:50, after two splits of the native run with one pair per interaction).** That construction gives 20.1 pairs per user (every turn of a multi-turn dialogue is an interaction), whereas LoRe reports about 3.9 few-shot pairs per unseen user, which matches one pair per *dialogue* (about seven dialogues per user, half of them few-shot). The native setting is therefore redefined as **one pair per conversation, from its opening turn (turn 0)**, with the per-user train/test halves taken over conversations; the per-interaction run is completed and reported as a variant.
