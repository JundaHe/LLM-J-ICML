## Reading (written after the run; the rule is Amendment 4's, fixed before it)

**G1 does not survive as a general claim.** Under the fixed rule (helps against no adaptation *and* beats every generic arm fitted on the same n pool-B ratings) G1 holds in 4 / 5 / 7 of 43 real pool cells at n = 20 / 50 / all, and in 3 / 5 / 3 of the 15 cells where a pool shift is shown.

- **Against target-only fitting the source-pool map wins almost everywhere, and that is not evidence of adaptation.** map-B(n) is ahead of climatology, both binnings and the map refitted without the pool-A prior in 39–43 of 43 real cells at n = 20 and 50 — and in 5 of 5 null cells. Twenty to fifty ratings cannot support a calibrator fitted from scratch; any use of the source pool beats it.
- **The competitor that matters is `tmix-B(n)`** (head-A's distribution, a temperature and a mixture with the n ratings' own climatology: two numbers). v3's `dir-B` mixed with pool A's climatology and could not absorb a shift in the marginal; that is where v3's large margins came from. Against tmix-B, map-B(n) is better / not separated / worse in 30 / 10 / 3 real cells at n = 20, 17 / 22 / 4 at n = 50, 18 / 22 / 3 at n = all; where it is better the margin is 0.001–0.003 RPS on the large-shift pools (v3's margin over dir-B there was 0.02–0.03).

  The v3 effect against no adaptation replicates with intervals (the shift is real and 20–50 ratings recover 51–93 % of it). What does not replicate is the advantage over a generic two-number recalibration: at n = 20 tmix-B is ahead on the two largest shifts, at n = 50 the map is ahead by about 0.002–0.003 (≈ 5–8 % of the gap).
- **Adapting blindly costs.** On the 28 real cells without a shown shift, map-B(n) reads *hurts* (worse than `none` or map-A) in 16 at n = 20 and 19 at n = 50 (median +0.0001 and +0.0028); on the random-halves nulls it *hurts* in 4 of 5 and 5 of 5. tmix-B is worse still there (worse than `none` in 23 and 17 of 28). Neither arm is safe without first knowing that a shift exists, and 20–50 ratings do not tell.
- **The null is not clean.** none − oracle-B has an interval above 0 on 2 of 5 random-halves nulls (DICES-990 +0.0014, DICES-350 +0.0033), and at n = all reading (i) says *helps* on 3 of 5 nulls (one, POPQUORN politeness, passes all of G1 with −0.0004). The interval covers item sampling only; random halves of 52–86 persistent raters differ in their rater mix, and the instrument reads that as a shift. Real shifts of that order (DICES-990 Gen Z +0.0016, POPQUORN politeness Man +0.0017, BW cs101 +0.0040, DICES-350 Black +0.0043) are therefore not established; the four pools in the table are 5–30 times the null values. A null matched to each pool's rater count was not run.
- **Against the expectation recorded in the amendment.** (a) held. (b) half wrong: I expected the map to stay ahead of tmix-B at n = 20–50 on the four large rows; it does at n = 50 on the three DICES-990 rows; at n = 20 only on US locale. (c) held: 15 of 43 real pools show a shift, and outside them adaptation is neutral or harmful.

What can be claimed: on DICES-990, rater pools defined by race or locale rate measurably differently from the largest pool, and 50 target-pool ratings recover 82–93 % of that difference; a two-number recalibration of the source head does nearly as well, and better at 20 ratings on the largest shifts. That is a finding about pool shift in one dataset, not an adaptation method. Departure from v3 not in the amendment: a pool-B split is skipped below 20 calibration ratings (v3: 10), since n = 20 is the smallest size; the pool set is the same 43 real + 5 null pools as v3. One cell has too few splits for a verdict (POPQUORN politeness, education "Other", n = 50).
