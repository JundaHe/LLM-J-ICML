# Research Proposal: Can Small Annotator Panels Tell Whether Soft-Label LLM Judges Learn Disagreement? An Identifiability Audit of Distributional Judge Training in Software Engineering

*Final version after 5 Codex GPT-5.6 refinement rounds (final verdict REVISE, 7.6/10) **and the external review's round-2 corrections** (Codex ultra, PROCEED WITH CAUTION): the support quantity is now the max-changed fraction under randomised maximum-cardinality matching on the hash-grouped split (commit Why .33–.37 at exact w = 1 — exact randomisation IS feasible on the primary criterion); exact w = 1 is primary on commit and readability, w = 2 sensitivity only; a matched no-adversary arm is mandatory; C1 is narrowed to finite-sample feasibility of this intervention; gates are correctness/power, not outcome. Remaining weaknesses are listed at the end and carried into `EXPERIMENT_PLAN.md`.*

## Problem Anchor
- **Bottom-line problem**: Training-side claims that an LLM judge "models human disagreement" (soft-label / distributional fine-tuning, Chen et al. NeurIPS 2025 and successors) are validated against the same pooled rating histogram they were trained on. On the small annotator panels that software-engineering benchmarks actually have (3–9 raters), we need a way to tell whether such a judge learned *which artifacts* split human raters, or merely learned a better-calibrated (smoother) score distribution plus scale-location effects.
- **Must-solve bottleneck**: The existing evaluation contract cannot distinguish item-specific disagreement learning from global smoothing; a judge can post large KL/RPS gains over point-SFT while carrying zero item-level disagreement information, and uncertainty-based downstream decisions (routing contested items to humans) inherit that ambiguity.
- **Non-goals**: Not a new judge-training method as the headline; not pairwise preference / reward-model training; not RL judges; not a new multi-rater benchmark; not claims about human populations beyond the released panels; not generation-side (DPO/best-of-n) consequences.
- **Constraints**: Released per-annotator data only (commit messages 800×6, code summarization 1,163×3, readability 200×9); 7B–14B open judges with LoRA on 4 shared RTX 6000 Ada (~22 GB free each) plus small OpenRouter budget for frontier judges as *untrained* references; single PhD student, ~3 months to submission; venue ICSE/FSE (primary) or ACL/NeurIPS Datasets & Benchmarks; parent paper's CSJE/HCA machinery is reusable and must stay consistent with it.
- **Success condition**: A protocol (target-reliability diagnostic → location-aware nulls → label-preserving attribution against held-out individual raters by consensus stratum) that, applied to Chen-style distributional judges on the three SE panels with ≥ 3 seeds and ≥ 2 model families, yields a definite, replicated verdict on whether the distributional gain is item-specific — and that a sceptical reviewer accepts as the right way to audit future "disagreement-aware judge" claims. A positive item-specific residual on some criterion/panel and a null on others is an acceptable (indeed informative) outcome.

## Technical Gap
Soft-label judge training (Chen et al. 2505.12301: α·KL to the empirical histogram + (1−α)·CE to the majority label + an adversarial ℓ₂-ball term; Li et al. 2510.00263; Singh et al. 2511.14117) is scored against the *same pooled histogram* it was fitted to. Three facts make that score uninterpretable as evidence of "learned disagreement": (1) a proper score decomposes into reliability − resolution + uncertainty, and a globally calibrated point-label judge can match a soft-label judge on RPS with identical resolution — in our commit-message pilot the raw CE→KL gain (+0.004 What / +0.011 Why RPS) shrinks to +0.0008 [−0.0001, +0.0017] / +0.0018 [+0.0005, +0.0031] once the same two-parameter calibrator is fitted to both arms; (2) on bounded ordinal scales the Low-consensus stratum is predictable from the pooled median alone (AUROC .67/.80), so entropy-based "disagreement detection" is mostly location; (3) averaging a held-out-rating score over all raters *is* the pooled-histogram score plus a model-free constant, so "held-out rater" must mean held-out rater *identity*, excluded from every target and calibrator. The natural instrument — train on histograms randomly re-assigned among artifacts that share the point label and the rating sum, so that only the artifact→shape link is broken — requires two artifacts with *different* histograms in the same (label, sum) cell. Our support audit (hash-grouped split, randomised maximum-cardinality matching) shows the intervention has *incomplete intensity* on the panels SE actually has: a maximum exact matching can change ≈ 25–32 % of training artifacts on commit What, 33–37 % on commit Why, 47–59 % on readability, and exactly 0 with 2 target raters (a ≤ b ⇒ z_i = a, b = Σy − z_i); changed items receive a mean total-variation dose of ≈ 0.40 (exact) vs ≈ 0.25 (w = 2). What is missing is therefore a statement of the *finite-sample feasibility of this intervention* — distinguishing structural impossibility (0 changeable), partial intensity (a fraction changeable, with positive per-item change probability under randomised matching) and the minimum detectable effect that intensity and dose afford — and an audit scoped to what the intervention can support.

## Method Thesis
- **One-sentence thesis**: On the small annotator panels of released SE benchmarks, the exact mode-, mean- and pool-preserving target-link randomisation that would attribute a soft-label judge's gain to item-linked histogram information has **limited but non-zero finite-sample intervention intensity** (≈ 0.25–0.37 of training artifacts changeable on commit messages, ≈ 0.5 on readability, 0 with two target raters); we state this feasibility result, and estimate — with symmetric calibration, excluded rater identities and explicit intensities — the Chen-package gain Δ_soft, its no-adversary decomposition, and the partial-ITT exact Δ_link^ITT(G) on commit Why/What and readability, leaving what the results license to a results-conditioned claim matrix.
- **Why this is the smallest adequate intervention**: no model, no loss; a CPU support enumeration with a stated concentration model, plus three training arms differing only in the distributional target, one symmetric calibration step and one evaluator rotation, stage-gated on the enumeration.
- **Why this route is timely**: soft-label / distributional judges and reward models are proliferating; the community needs to know which of their claims the available panels can support at all.

## Contribution Focus
- **Dominant contribution**: the finite-sample feasibility analysis of the exact target-link intervention — (i) the estimand Δ_link^ITT(G) for the feasible partial intervention G = a randomised maximum-cardinality exact matching within heterogeneous cells (pool, z_i, Σy) (identity elsewhere), with three support quantities (full-cell derangeability; max-changed fraction = Σ_cells min{N, 2(N−M)}/n; per-item positive change probability) and the treatment dose (TV distance between original and donor histogram), and the statement of what positive and null results license (a positive effect is evidence of item-linked histogram information *beyond mode and mean*; a null at partial intensity does not establish absence); (ii) an empirical support audit on the three SE panels under grouped and leave-one-rater-out rotations with subsampled-rater curves, plus a *frozen* support model (cell occupancy × histogram-class frequencies × matching key) validated on held-out grouped splits and synthetic regimes — or, if it does not validate, reported as an empirical audit only; (iii) the two-rater lemma (with two target ratings, (z_i, Σy) determines the histogram under the stated tie rule); (iv) MDE/power simulations that turn intensity and dose into minimum detectable Δ_link per panel, replacing any fixed coverage threshold.
- **Optional supporting contribution**: the scoped audit — the Chen-package gain Δ_soft (excluded raters, symmetric arm-specific calibration) and its decomposition into the no-adversary objective gain and the adversarial-term effect; exact Δ_link^ITT(G) on commit Why (primary; max-changed .33–.37, dose .40), commit What (.25–.32) and readability (.47–.59; dispersion reliability on the 124 keyed snippets ≈ −0.04, so a null there is uninformative); Δ_bin-link (w = 2) as a named bounded-confounding sensitivity contrast only; five-bin REL/RES/UNC as descriptive diagnostics; the protocol comparison P0/P1/P2; the pilot's observation that the Chen-style core's gain shrank to +0.0008 / +0.0018 RPS after symmetric calibration (to be re-estimated under the final protocol, not quoted as a conclusion).
- **Explicit non-contributions**: no new training objective; no routing; no metric claims; no panel-size law; no population claims beyond the released panels; no claim of general non-identifiability (only finite-sample feasibility of this intervention); Δ_bin-link is neither an upper bound nor identified conditional resolution; REL/RES/UNC are descriptive, not a causal share; Δ_reg appears only as an algebraic residual in the appendix.

## Proposed Method
### Complexity Budget
- Frozen / reused: Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct; verbatim single-criterion prompts (Zeng WHAT / WHY zero-shot templates; CoReEval readability prompt); LoRA r = 16, α_LoRA = 32, dropout 0.05 on q/k/v/o/gate/up/down; parent CSJE strata code (strata from target raters).
- New trainable components: none.
- Tempting additions intentionally not used: posterior-predictive / CRPS / tie-aware training arms; per-rater conditioning; RL judges; routing heads; frontier fine-tuning; non-SE benchmarks; untrained frontier-judge rows; isotonic calibration branches; drift-stratified "deconfounding"; ten-bin sensitivities; summarization GPU runs.

### Core Mechanism
- Input / output: artifact + single-criterion prompt → q_A(x) ∈ Δ⁴; evaluator = a human rating whose rater identity was excluded from every target and calibrator.
- Architecture or policy: unchanged judge. The mechanism is the *design*: (i) the support condition and enumeration decide which contrast a panel can support and with what intensity; (ii) G_f preserves z_i (the CE class), pool and the rating sum (exact, w = 1) or sum bin (w = 2, |drift| ≤ 1/m_t) for every training artifact and replaces the histogram shape only in eligible cells; (iii) symmetric arm-specific calibration removes calibration differences representable by the (T, λ) family (residual REL reported; conclusions scoped to that family); (iv) evaluator-identity exclusion removes panel memorisation.
- Training signal / loss: the exact Chen objective with (α, ε*, inner steps, optimiser) from the released configuration; L_adv by projected gradient ascent on p′ ∈ Δ⁴ ∩ B_ε*(p) (ℓ₂-ball clipping then Euclidean simplex projection); identical steps, schedules, LoRA init and data order across arms within a replicate; if a released value is unavailable, the paper's ablation value is used and varied in a sensitivity row.
- Why this is the main novelty: Chen/Li evaluate on the fitted histogram; Gheibi 2605.20642 shuffles globally (destroying the label); Kohli 2605.29797 holds out an annotator group without calibration symmetry or link randomisation; Calderon 2501.10970 is leave-one-annotator-out for point judges. None states when a label-and-location-preserving randomisation can exist, and none reports that on typical SE panels it essentially cannot. The novelty must rest on the general, predictive treatment of target-randomisation positivity (enumeration + concentration model + lemma), not on the matching condition itself.

### Optional Supporting Component
- Pre-audit diagnostics (CPU, one table): split-half Spearman of per-item dispersion across disjoint rater halves per criterion; the support map and predicted-vs-observed κ. Used to interpret the estimates, never as a law.

### Modern Primitive Usage
- Open-weight instruction LLM judge (score-token distribution from logits) with LoRA — the audited object; intentionally non-frontier on the method side; no RL, DPO, verbalised probabilities or frontier fine-tuning.

### Integration into Base Generator / Downstream Pipeline
Plugs into the parent CSJE pipeline (strata from target raters; same distance conventions). Inference = one forward pass per item per criterion; calibration = two scalars per arm.

### Training Plan
- Split: one immutable grouped split per panel (seed 0); replicate seeds s ∈ {0, 1, 2} vary LoRA init, data order and G_f only.
- Rotations: commit LORO (6 folds, 5 target raters); readability LORO (9 folds, 8 target raters); summarization: analytic boundary only (no GPU runs).
- Criteria: commit What, Why (single-criterion prompts); readability (one criterion).
- Runs: commit 4 arms × 6 LORO folds × 3 outer splits × 2 families × 2 criteria = 288 (≈ 0.45 GPU-h → 130 GPU-h); readability 3 arms (A_chen0 nice-to-have) × 9 × 3 × 2 = 162 (≈ 0.1 → 16 GPU-h) → **450 runs ≈ 146 GPU-h** (+ ≈ 6 for the (α, ε) sensitivity row; + ≈ 11 for the w = 2 sensitivity on Why, Qwen, one split); ≈ 1.5 weeks on 4 shared cards. Stage-gated on **correctness and power, never on outcome**: Stage 0 (support audit script released; frozen support model; MDE simulation) → M1 = the exact commit-Why gate (4 arms × 6 folds × Qwen × split 0 = 24 runs ≈ 11 GPU-h) checked for parity with the released Chen implementation, invariants (no evaluator leakage; certification), and achieved MDE — then the rest is launched.
- Calibration: grid T ∈ {0.5, 0.7, 0.85, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6} × λ ∈ {0, .05, .1, .2, .3, .5} on commit (10 % calibration split); on readability the calibration split is raised to 15 % and the grid coarsened to T ∈ {0.7, 1, 1.5, 2, 3} × λ ∈ {0, .1, .3} because ≈ 12–19 calibration snippets cannot select among 60 settings; p_clim = target-rater level marginal over training items; objective = mean RPS over target raters on calibration items; fitted once per arm × fold × split × family; calibration refitting uncertainty reported by bootstrapping the calibration set.
- Pooling: Δ for a (panel, criterion, family) = mean over LORO folds of item-level paired differences within an outer split, bootstrapped over that split's test artifacts; the three splits give three estimates whose spread is the replication uncertainty; per-(fold, split) estimates with intensities and dose in Figure 2; families never pooled.

### Failure Modes and Diagnostics
- The MDE simulation shows Δ_link is undetectable at the achievable intensity/dose for a panel → that panel's Δ_link is reported as under-powered, not run; summarization has zero intensity by the lemma.
- Δ_soft ≈ 0 after calibration everywhere → "no soft-label gain beyond representable calibration" — still the paper.
- Δ_link^ITT positive on commit Why → evidence of item-linked histogram information beyond mode and mean at the realised intensity; null → not evidence of absence (partial intensity); readability null → uninformative (dispersion reliability on the 124 keyed snippets ≈ −0.04); Δ_bin-link read only as a composite sensitivity.
- Residual REL after calibration → reported; conclusions scoped to the (T, λ) family.
- Readability universe: locked to the 124 keyed CoReEval snippets (split-half dispersion reliability recomputed on them: ≈ −0.04 raw); the 76 snippets without released code are excluded and documented; the parent alignment bug is fixed upstream first.

### Novelty and Elegance Argument
One estimand with a support condition, one enumeration with a predictive model, one lemma, one support map; three arms differing only in the distributional target; one calibration step; one rotation. The contribution is the *identifiability* result and the disciplined audit it licenses — a measurement paper in the parent paper's lineage (CSJE/HCA → what training-side disagreement claims small panels can support), not a salami slice: new question, new instrument, new boundary.
