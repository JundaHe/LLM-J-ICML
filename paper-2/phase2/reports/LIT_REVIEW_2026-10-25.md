## C. Pluralistic alignment / preference distributions (positioning, not competitors)
- *Pairwise Calibrated Rewards for Pluralistic Alignment* (2506.06298) [snip]; *Hidden Consensus: Preference-Validity Compression* (2606.10569) [abs]: plural-valid answers collapsed by RLHF; *Welfare-Aware Preference Acquisition* (2610.19035) [snip]; MixDPO (2026) [snip]. Our message for this audience: with few raters, the *distributional* signal beyond location is mostly sampling noise on ordinal scales — pluralistic methods need either many raters or stable structure.

## D. Prior art for the candidate method (reliability-shrunk targets)
- Label smoothing as shrinkage / noise mitigation (Lukasik et al., ICML 2020) [snip]; class-conditional noise identifiability (Scott & Blanchard) [snip]; crowd soft-label aggregation for uncertainty under shift (PMC12148080) [snip]; noisy-label calibration with multiple unreliable labels (2610.19543, Oct 2026) [snip]; Ushio, Ishida & Sugiyama (ICLR 2026) — Bayes-error estimation with corrupted soft labels via isotonic calibration [abs]; Dawid–Skene / annotator models (classic).
- **Gap we found:** no work shrinks the *residual* of an empirical rating distribution toward a *statistic-preserving (mode+mean) comparator* with the shrinkage weight estimated from *cross-panel residual agreement* (λ* = X/M, from the exact identity A1 = M − 2X). Closest in spirit: label smoothing (shrinks toward uniform, tuned), empirical-Bayes multinomial smoothing (Dirichlet prior, not statistic-preserving). Novelty plausible but **needs a Codex + targeted search pass before claiming**.

## E. Implications for the ICML plan
1. The workshop paper by Gheibi & Ghazizadeh at ICML 2026 means the ICML community already finds "soft labels with few annotators are noisy" interesting — and that a main-track paper must go beyond it: decomposition (what part), mechanism (why: reliability), and a usable fix.
2. Our strongest differentiators: exact statistic-preserving decomposition with dose certificates; ordinal *and* nominal; end-to-end LLM judges; learner-free finite-rater identity linking annotation-side signal to learner contrasts.
3. Weakest points a reviewer will hit: null-centred headline; one base model family; POPQUORN rater-pool caveat; failed 1/a prediction; no positive method.

## F. Full-text reading of the closest papers (added 2026-10-25, after the user asked whether they were read carefully)
Earlier rows above were abstract-level. The following were read in full (HTML / PDF text) and checked for conflict with our results.

**F1. Gheibi & Ghazizadeh (EIML @ ICML 2026; 2605.20642).** CIFAR-10H, ResNet-18. Sparse condition: K ∈ {5, 10, 25, 50} labels per image drawn by multinomial sampling from the full CIFAR-10H distribution. Hard-label delivery (SLS, multipass, deterministic control) has lower soft NLL than soft-label CE at every K (e.g. K = 5: 0.586 soft vs 0.560 SLS) — but "none survives Holm correction over the 12-cell family". With the full distribution, soft ≈ SLS (NLL 0.5096 vs 0.5052, p = 0.70). Shuffled SLS = ONE global permutation of whole distributions across images (mode, mean, everything shuffled) → 12 % accuracy. Mechanism claimed: SLS adds gradient covariance Diag(p) − ppᵀ → flatter basins.
→ **No conflict.** Their sparse result is concordant with ours (with few labels the empirical target is noisy; our learner-free A1x > 0 at 5–9 raters). Their shuffle is a much cruder control (destroys the label itself); ours preserves mode / mean and certifies dose — the decomposition they do not do. Differences in setting: image classification vs ordinal/nominal text, ResNet vs LLM judges, optimisation mechanism vs annotation-side reliability. Their weak significance (no Holm survivor) leaves room for our sharper, certified evidence.

**F2. Kohli, Metric-Dependent Annotation Saturation (2605.29797).** ChaosNLI (100 raters, 3 classes), DeBERTa-v3-base + RoBERTa-large, KL loss; N ∈ {3, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100}, 5 × 5 seeds; DICES-990 binary exploratory. KL saturates by N ≈ 10, entropy correlation needs 20–50; soft labels beat uniform label smoothing (entropy corr 0.643 vs ≤ 0.49). No item permutation, no ordinal data, no mode/mean-preserving control.
→ **No conflict; complementary.** Same scale as our ChaosNLI finite-rater curves (raw histogram beats the T-comparator from 5–10 raters; reference crossover ≈ 14–15) — a learner-side and a learner-free view of the same phenomenon. Our additions: ordinal panels (where the residual never wins through 40 raters on DICES), exact statistic-preserving controls, LLM judges. Reviewer may say "annotation-count dependence is known" → answer: known for nominal entropy ranking; we show it for the residual beyond mode+mean, on ordinal scales, with an exact decomposition.

**F3. Singh et al., Distributions In, Distributions Out (2511.14117).** Frozen text/image embeddings + 2-layer MLP (same pipeline family as our heads); ChaosNLI, POPQUORN (treated as 5-class nominal, ~6.7 raters), CIFAR-10H-Hard; soft vs majority-vote hard only; no smoothing or shuffle control. POPQUORN: accuracy 51.98 vs 52.00, entropy correlation +24 %.
→ **No conflict.** They compare soft vs hard; our hard arm is also worse than soft. Our audit explains their POPQUORN gain: mode+mean-preserving targets match soft (heads +0.0002; LoRA +0.0000). Worth one sentence: "the soft-over-hard gains reported by Singh et al. are reproduced by location-matched targets on the ordinal panels we test."

**F4. Benito-Santos & Ghajari, Beyond Averages (EMNLP 2025).** Soft-STS-15 (7,890 pairs, 0–5 ordinal). Two methods: (a) a truncated-Gaussian head = predicted mean + a variance σ calibrated per mode × disagreement-tercile slice; (b) a cross-encoder trained on soft labels with an ordinal loss + slice-wise temperature scaling. Gains in δ-EMD (calibration) over a mean-regression baseline trained on STS-B.
→ **No conflict; mildly supportive.** Their best-performing light method is itself a location + calibrated-spread model — no shape beyond mean/variance is tested. Consistent with our location-sufficiency reading on ordinal scales.

**F5. Pavlovic, Paun & Poesio (2605.18648).** MNIST re-annotations (~6 raters/image, 480 annotators) and Ambiguous-MNIST; FFNs / LeNet; soft (equal / uncertainty-weighted) vs new majority vote (mode-preserving hard). Soft labels improve KLD (e.g. 1.661 → 0.669) and confidence–uncertainty correlation; "larger value lies in acting as a regularizer that improves model calibration on difficult samples".
→ **No conflict; supportive** (calibration, not shape). Their mode-preserving control is hard (removes all spread), ours keeps mean and certifies the residual change.

**F6. Behrens & Zdeborová (ICLR 2026; 2506.14457).** Dataset distillation of *teacher* soft labels; held-out memorised data can be recovered. Different object (model outputs, memorisation), not human disagreement. Low conflict; cite only for the within-class reassignment control if used.

**Bottom line.** None of the closest papers contradicts our findings; three are concordant (F1, F2, F5) and one is compatible/supportive (F4). The overlap risk is in *message* ("few labels make soft targets noisy", "annotation count matters"), not in results. Our defensible deltas: exact statistic-preserving decomposition with dose certificates; ordinal + nominal; end-to-end LLM judges; a learner-free finite-rater identity (A1 = M − 2X) that explains when the residual can help.
