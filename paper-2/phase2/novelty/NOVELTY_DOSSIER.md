## Proposed method / contribution (as the paper states it)
A support- and dose-certified donor-replacement audit of distributional ("soft-label") supervision for learned judges. For each training item, the empirical rating histogram is replaced by a donor histogram from another training item within a cell that retains specified statistics — the modal class only (pmode) or mode and mean (pmm; pmmx with an exact KKT-certified KL projection to the recipient's mode and mean). Training everything else unchanged, the raw proper-score contrast (RPS vs held-out annotation positions; KL for nominal) estimates the average predictive consequence of disrupting the item↔histogram association while retaining those statistics. Every contrast is accompanied by a certificate: support (fraction of items in cells with ≥ 2 histogram classes), realised changed fraction, TV among changed items, TV over all items, zero-TV pairs, post-projection dose. Three estimands (raw, common-map calibrated, arm-specific calibrated). Applied to frozen-feature heads on 5–7 ordinal panels (SummEval coherence, Scalabrino, Buse–Weimer, DICES, HelpSteer2 [+ commit What/Why, being removed]) and one nominal panel (ChaosNLI, m ∈ {5,10,20,50}), and to LoRA-fine-tuned 7B judges with the Chen et al. (NeurIPS 2025) hybrid KL+CE+adversarial objective on SummEval coherence (3 joint optimizer–assignment seeds × 3 expert folds).
Findings: (1) exact mode-and-mean-preserving replacement changed average RPS by −0.0024…+0.0005 on ordinal head panels; on SummEval LoRA (81 % of targets changed, TV 0.34) the raw contrast is −0.0001 [−0.0011, +0.0011]; (2) mode-only replacement increased RPS on SummEval by +0.0120 [+0.0086, +0.0155] in all nine seed×fold cells and on several head panels; (3) on nominal ChaosNLI, scrambling residual class allocation while preserving modal class and mass increased KL by +0.016…+0.029 at every annotation count; (4) no constructed target (SORD, mode-conditional prototype, projected prototype, cross-fitted conditional reference, shrinkage) had the lowest observed RPS on every panel; (5) semi-synthetic positive control shows injected item-linked shape is learnable and removed by the replacement.

## Core claims whose novelty must be judged
C1. The audit design itself: statistic-preserving donor replacement of training label distributions (within-cell constrained derangement on histogram classes; exact projection variant) as a way to ask WHICH properties of the histogram a distributional learner uses — as opposed to (a) soft vs hard, (b) soft vs label smoothing, (c) full-label permutation / shuffling.
C2. The support / changed-fraction / TV dose certificates as a necessary companion to any supervision-ablation null, and the finding that on 3–5-rating panels mode and mean leave little residual support.
C3. The empirical finding: on ordinal panels the learner's measurable benefit from the histogram is carried by location (mode+mean); residual allocation beyond mode and mean produced no detectable average benefit at the available doses, while on nominal ChaosNLI residual allocation did carry signal.
C4. The three-estimand (raw / common calibration / arm calibration) reporting with crossed bootstrap over joint optimizer–assignment realisations, and the exact KKT active-set KL projection onto {mode, mean} constraints as a certified target construction.
C5. The negative result that no two-statistic constructed target dominates the empirical histogram.

## Candidate prior work found by web search (verify each; add any we missed)
- Chen et al. 2025, "Beyond Single-Point Judgment: Distribution Alignment for LLM-as-a-Judge", NeurIPS 2025, arXiv 2505.12301 — the training objective we audit (KL + CE + adversarial). They do not ask which histogram statistics are used.
- "Distributions In, Distributions Out: The Case for Soft-Label Training" (arXiv 2511.14117; ChaosNLI, POPQUORN, CIFAR-10H) — soft vs hard with a label-smoothing control; entropy correlation; no statistic-preserving intervention (as far as the abstract shows).
- "Metric-Dependent Annotation Saturation for Learning from Label Distributions" (arXiv 2605.29797; ChaosNLI; subsampled annotation counts N ~ 10–50; soft vs smoothing; "soft labels carry item-specific signal that label smoothing cannot replicate") — overlaps our ChaosNLI m-sweep in design (annotation subsampling) but intervenes by subsampling, not by statistic-preserving replacement.
- "Post-hoc Alignment of LLM-judges to Human Judgment Distribution" (NAPHA; arXiv 2609.01073) — post-hoc, entropy-class routing; not a supervision audit.
- Elangovan et al., ICLR 2025 — LLM-judge evaluation under human rating uncertainty / indeterminacy (also arXiv 2503.05965 "Validating LLM-as-a-Judge Systems under Rating Indeterminacy").
- "Quantifying and Predicting Disagreement in Graded Human Ratings" (arXiv 2605.01168) — predicting full Likert distributions with ordinal objectives (EMD, EMD + mean regularisation); may contain a mean-vs-distribution comparison.
- "LLMs Capture Emotion Labels, Not Emotion Uncertainty" (arXiv 2604.27345) — GoEmotions/EmoBank distributional analysis; post-hoc calibration.
- LeWiDi-2025 (arXiv 2510.08460) shared task; Uma et al. 2021 survey; Plank 2022; Peterson et al. 2019 (CIFAR-10H); Zhou, Nie & Bansal 2022 "Distributed NLI"; Geng 2016 LDL; Díaz & Marathe 2019 SORD; Wen et al. ICCV 2023 ordinal LDL; "Unimodal-Concentrated Loss" (2204.00309); D3O (2607.23575).
- Methodological precedents for constrained permutation controls: Ojala & Garriga 2010 (JMLR) permutation tests for classifiers — Test 2 permutes features within class (conditional permutation); Zhang et al. 2017 label randomisation; permutation feature importance (Breiman 2001; Fisher et al. 2019); conditional randomisation tests / knockoffs (Candès et al. 2018); degree-preserving randomisation in network science.
