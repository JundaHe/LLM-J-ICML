# A Soft Label Is Worth Two Numbers: What Distributional Judge Training Actually Learns  (working title, 2026-10-11)

Target: ACL/EMNLP main (alt. NeurIPS). Audience: human-label-variation / LLM-as-a-judge community.

## One-paragraph claim
Distributional ("soft-label") fine-tuning of LLM judges — fitting the full histogram of human ratings instead of the majority label (Chen et al. 2025) — is
reported as teaching judges "human disagreement". We show, with a label-preserving permutation audit and a two-number control target, that on ordinal
rating panels the item-level information a soft label transmits is its **mean**: (i) permuting histograms across items while preserving mode and mean costs
nothing (LoRA judges on 7B models; small heads on frozen features; five panels), (ii) permuting while preserving only the mode destroys the soft-label gain
and makes soft labels worse than hard labels, (iii) a target built from (mode, mean) alone matches the histogram everywhere and beats it on 4–6-rater panels,
where the histogram's shape is sampling noise, and (iv) shape beyond the mean only becomes recoverable at ~100 raters and is then learned only partially.
A semi-synthetic positive control shows the audit detects item-linked shape when it exists. The results reinterpret Kohli et al. (2026)'s
annotation-saturation curves (KL saturates at ~10 raters because the mean does; entropy needs 20–50 because shape does), and give practitioners a cheaper
target: collect enough raters to estimate the item mean; do not fit the histogram.

## Update 2026-10-11 05:00 — seven panels, three domains, one decomposition
Panels: commit What / Why (SE, 800×6), Scalabrino readability (124×9), Buse–Weimer (100×121), DICES-350 safety (350×104, K=3), **HelpSteer2 helpfulness (4,786×5, K=5)**, **SummEval coherence / relevance (1,600×8, K=5)**.
Finding sharpened: Δ_soft decomposes per task into (a) class-conditional smoothing (commit What, HelpSteer2) and (b) item-mean information (commit Why, SummEval coherence, DICES, BW); the residual histogram shape is never used (mode+mean permutation inert on 7/7 panels), and a (mode, mean) target matches or beats the histogram everywhere. Framing: "disagreement-aware" training transmits at most two numbers per item; on 4–6-rater panels the histogram's shape is noise that hurts.

## Contributions
1. **Two-level link-randomisation audit** (mode-only vs mode+mean permutation) that decomposes a distributional-training gain into global smoothing, item-linked mean, and residual shape — with treatment-intensity certificates and a positive control (assay sensitivity).
2. **Empirical finding across five panels** (commit What/Why 800×6, Scalabrino readability 124×9, Buse–Weimer 100×121, DICES-350 350×104; K=3/5; LoRA 7B judges + frozen-feature heads): soft-label gains are mean-carried; mode+mean permutation is inert; mode-only permutation is destructive.
3. **Sufficiency of (mode, mean)**: a two-parameter target (mode-conditional prototype exponentially tilted to the item mean) matches or beats the histogram as a training target, and beats tuned label smoothing — so the gain is item-specific information, but only two numbers of it.
4. **When shape matters**: oracle-gap analysis (reference-rater D with vs without shape) shows beyond-mean disagreement information appears only at ~100 raters on 5-level scales, is absent on 3-level scales by construction, and is recovered at ≈14 % by learners from 60 items — a quantitative learnability account complementing Kohli's saturation curves.
5. **Negative result on rater conditioning** (Stage A): putting rater identity/history into the input buys severity (a per-rater shift that a 4-rating prior reproduces), not item×rater interaction; margin-preserving interaction scramble is inert; seen-ID oracle no better than additive. Bounds what "perspectivist" judges can learn from 100-rater crossed panels.

## Method section — draft (2026-10-11 07:00)

### Setting
A panel of $m$ raters scores each item $i$ on an ordinal scale $\{0,\dots,K-1\}$, giving a rating vector $h_i\in\{0..K-1\}^m$ and an empirical histogram $p_i$ (the "soft label"). A judge $q_\theta(y\mid x_i)$ is trained on the training items and evaluated against a rater who never contributed to any training target or calibration fit (leave-one-rater-out, LORO). Distributional training (Chen et al. 2025) minimises $\alpha\,\mathrm{KL}(p'_i\,\|\,q_\theta)+(1-\alpha)\,\mathrm{CE}(z_i,q_\theta)$ with $z_i$ the mode and $p'_i$ an adversarial perturbation of $p_i$ inside an $\ell_2$ ball; we keep this objective fixed and vary only the target.

### The target hierarchy (what information each target carries)
| target | carries | definition |
|---|---|---|
| hard | mode | one-hot $z_i$ |
| smooth | mode + global ε | $(1-\varepsilon)\,\mathbb 1[z_i]+\varepsilon/K$, ε tuned on validation items |
| proto | mode + class-conditional shape | $\bar p_{z_i}$ = mean training histogram of items with mode $z_i$ |
| **mean2** | mode + item mean | $\bar p_{z_i}(y)\,e^{\lambda_i y}/Z_i$ with $\lambda_i$ chosen so that $\mathbb E[y]=\bar h_i$ (exponential tilting; unique by monotonicity) |
| soft | mode + mean + shape | $p_i$ |
| soft_pmode | mode (+ *another item's* mean and shape) | $p_{G(i)}$, $G$ a derangement within mode cells |
| soft_pmm | mode + mean (+ *another item's* shape) | $p_{G(i)}$, $G$ a derangement within (mode, mean-bucket of width τ) cells |

Permutations are randomised maximum-cardinality matchings within cells on the training items only; every run writes a certificate: fraction of items changed, mean total-variation dose, and the realised mean shift ($|\bar h_i-\bar h_{G(i)}|$, zero up to τ for pmm, ≈0.3–0.45 on a 0–4 scale for pmode).

Three contrasts decompose a soft-label gain Δ_soft = RPS(hard) − RPS(soft):
- **smoothing** = hard − proto (or hard − smooth)
- **item mean** = proto − mean2  (equivalently soft − soft_pmode minus the shape term)
- **shape** = mean2 − soft  (equivalently soft_pmm − soft)
Identification: soft_pmm − soft ≈ 0 ⇔ residual shape is not used; soft_pmode − soft > 0 ⇔ item-linked information beyond the mode is used; mean2 ≈ soft ⇔ that information is the mean.

### Evaluation
Per-arm symmetric calibration on the calibration items against the target raters only (temperature × climatology shrinkage grid), then RPS against the held-out rater on test items; KL to the held-out reference histogram where ≥ 2 reference raters exist; ordinal disagreement $D_i=\mathbb E|y-y'|/(K-1)$ from the reference raters vs the model's predicted $D$: Spearman, top-quartile AUROC, and partial Spearman given the reference item mean (the position-effect control). Item-clustered bootstrap (B = 2000), groups = source document where items share a source. Two model classes: LoRA-tuned 7B judges (score read from the next-token distribution) and small heads on frozen last-layer features of the same base model (5 seeds × 3 item splits × 2 rater splits).

### Positive control (assay sensitivity)
A semi-synthetic world in which item-linked shape exists: for items with PCA-1 feature above the training median, pairs of ratings $(y,y)\to(y-1,y+1)$ (disperse), below median $(y-1,y+1)\to(y,y)$ (concentrate), mode and mean preserved, applied to training and reference raters alike. On Buse–Weimer and Scalabrino the assay then shows soft > mean2 and soft_pmm < soft (learnable fraction 0.94–0.98); with ≤ 6 raters the moves are infeasible, i.e. there is no shape to carry — which is the regime of most released panels.

### Oracle gap
$\rho(D^{\text{oracle\_soft}},D^{\text{ref}})-\rho(D^{\text{oracle\_mean2}},D^{\text{ref}})$ computed from the training-rater histograms directly (no model): how much disagreement information the histogram shape could carry at best. ≈0 at 3–9 raters and on 3-level scales; 0.21 at 121 raters (K=5).

### R3. Assay sensitivity (positive control)
Injecting item-linked shape with mode and mean held fixed (Buse–Weimer, Scalabrino) makes soft − mean2 jump to +0.46 and +0.11 in disagreement Spearman and makes soft_pmm − soft negative (−0.20, −0.12), with learnable fractions 0.94–0.98 of the injected oracle gap. The same injection is infeasible on commit (4 raters), SummEval (6) and HelpSteer2 (3): with so few ratings per item there are no pairs to move without changing the mode or mean — which is exactly why released panels carry no shape.

### R4. When would shape matter? (oracle gap)
The histogram's best-case advantage over (mode, mean) for ranking items by disagreement is ≈ 0 at 3–9 raters (−0.03 … +0.01), 0 by construction on a 3-level scale (DICES: reference D is a deterministic function of the mean, R² = 1.00), and 0.21 at 121 raters on a 5-level scale (Buse–Weimer); learners recover ≈ 14 % of that from 60 items. This is the mechanism behind Kohli et al.'s metric-dependent saturation: KL saturates when the mean does; entropy needs the shape, which needs tens of raters.

### R5. Rater conditioning does not change the picture (Stage A)
Putting a rater's history into the input improves per-rater prediction (RPS 0.219 → 0.187 at 16 support ratings on DICES) but a history-only prior with no item information reaches 0.192; an item × rater interaction model is not better than an additive shift (+0.0017 ± 0.0012), a margin-preserving interaction scramble changes nothing, and a seen-rater ID oracle is no better. Identity buys severity, not item-specific disagreement.

## Introduction — draft (2026-10-11 09:00)

Human raters disagree, and a growing line of work argues that an LLM judge should be trained to reproduce that disagreement rather than a single majority label: fit the full histogram of human ratings with a KL objective, add an adversarial term, and report that the judge's predicted distributions match human distributions better than a hard-label judge does (Chen et al., 2025; follow-ups). The improvement is real — we reproduce it on eight rating panels and two model classes — and it is routinely interpreted as the judge having learned *which items people disagree about and how*.

This paper asks what information the histogram actually transmits to the judge. The question has a clean experimental answer because a histogram over an ordinal scale decomposes: its mode, its mean, and whatever shape remains once mode and mean are fixed. We train judges on targets that carry exactly one of these increments — a mode-conditional prototype (mode only, plus class-conditional smoothing), the prototype exponentially tilted to the item's mean (mode and mean: two numbers), and the full histogram — and we break specific links by permuting targets across items under constraints that preserve the mode alone or the mode and the mean. Each permutation carries a certificate (fraction of items changed, total-variation dose, realised mean shift), the evaluation rater never enters any target or calibration fit, and a semi-synthetic positive control shows the design detects item-linked shape when it exists.

The answer is the same on software-engineering, safety and summarisation panels, for 7B LoRA judges and for small heads on frozen features: **a soft label is worth at most two numbers.** Permuting histograms while preserving mode and mean never costs anything (8/8 panels; 7B judges on both commit criteria). Where the soft-label gain is item-specific it is carried by the mean — permuting while preserving only the mode destroys it and leaves soft labels worse than hard labels (commit *Why*: +0.0033 RPS, CI excludes 0) — and where it is not, a mode-only prototype matches or beats the histogram (commit *What*, HelpSteer2). A target built from (mode, mean) matches the histogram everywhere and beats it on 4–6-rater panels, where the histogram's residual shape is sampling noise that the judge pays for. Shape beyond the mean becomes recoverable only at ~100 raters on a 5-level scale (oracle gap 0.21 on Buse–Weimer) and is then learned only in part; on 3-level scales disagreement is a deterministic function of the mean and the question is moot (DICES, ChaosNLI-type data). Putting the *rater* into the input does not change the picture: identity buys a severity shift that four of the rater's own ratings already supply, not item × rater interaction.

These results explain the metric-dependent annotation saturation reported by Kohli et al. (2026) — distributional-fit metrics saturate when the item mean does, at ~10 raters; disagreement-identification metrics need the shape, which needs tens of raters — and they have a practical consequence: for the panel sizes that released SE and NLP benchmarks actually have, "disagreement-aware" training should target the item mean (two numbers per item), not the histogram, and claims that a judge has learned human disagreement should be required to survive a mode-and-mean-preserving permutation of their training targets.

Contributions: (1) a two-level link-randomisation audit with certificates and a positive control; (2) the empirical decomposition on eight panels / three domains / two model classes; (3) the two-number target as a drop-in replacement for histogram targets; (4) an oracle-gap analysis of when shape could matter; (5) a negative result on rater-conditioned judges.

## Discussion — draft (2026-10-11 10:00)

**What "learning disagreement" can mean on released panels.** Three quantities are routinely conflated: a judge's distributional fit (KL/RPS to the human histogram), its ability to rank items by how much humans disagree, and its knowledge of *who* disagrees. Our decomposition separates the first into global smoothing, item mean and residual shape, and shows that on panels of 3–9 raters only the first two exist in the data. Distributional-fit gains should therefore not be read as disagreement learning; they are a better-calibrated point estimate (the mean) plus class-conditional smoothing. Kohli et al.'s saturation curves follow: fit saturates when the mean does; identification of contested items requires the shape, which requires tens of raters.

**Practical consequence: collect means, not histograms.** For a fixed annotation budget, three raters per item already pin the mean well enough that a (mode, mean) target matches or beats the histogram as a training signal. Spending the budget on more items rather than more raters per item is the better trade for judge training — unless the goal is specifically to identify contested items, in which case tens of raters per item are needed and the judge should be evaluated on disagreement ranking conditional on the mean, not on KL.

**An evaluation contract for "disagreement-aware" judges.** Any claim that a judge has learned human disagreement should (i) survive a mode-and-mean-preserving permutation of its training targets (otherwise the claim is about the mean), (ii) report disagreement recovery conditional on the item mean (otherwise it is the position effect), (iii) include a positive control showing the pipeline can detect item-linked shape at the panel size used, and (iv) hold out raters, not just items.

**Why rater conditioning does not help here.** On fully crossed 100-rater panels the item × rater interaction is not learnable from frozen-feature heads or seen-ID oracles; what identity buys is severity. This is consistent with the oracle-gap result — most of the variance that is not item mean is unsystematic — and bounds what perspectivist judges can promise on panels of this kind. Demographic conditioning (DiADEM-style) is the natural follow-up; we expect it to recover group-level severity, not item-specific taste.

**Relation to the Phase-1 audit.** Our earlier finding that mode-and-mean-preserving permutation is inert on 3–9-rater SE panels was correct but under-identified: it could not distinguish "the gain is global" from "the gain is the mean". The mode-only arm and the two-number target resolve this, and the answer differs by task (What: smoothing; Why: mean), which the single-arm audit could not have revealed.

## Limitations — draft
- *Reference panels.* On commit, SummEval and HelpSteer2 the held-out reference is 1–2 raters (positions), so disagreement metrics are noisy and the oracle gap is undefined there; the shape-vs-mean conclusion on those panels rests on KL/RPS and on the permutation contrasts, not on D.
- *Positive controls* exist only where ≥ 9 raters allow mode- and mean-preserving moves (Buse–Weimer, Scalabrino); on 3–6-rater panels no such moves exist, which is itself the finding but leaves the assay untested in that regime.
- *LoRA evidence* is one base family on commit (DeepSeek replication running) and one on SummEval; splits 0–2 for Why; one optimiser seed per split. Heads provide 5 seeds × 3 × 2 on all panels.
- *Scales.* DICES is 3-level; HelpSteer2's 5-rating subset is disagreement-selected; SummEval turker positions are not fixed identities. Each is stated and used only for item-level analyses.
- *Objective.* We hold Chen et al.'s objective fixed (LoRA, reconstructed adversary); the adversarial term is inert in our runs, which may differ from full fine-tuning.
- *Scope.* Ordinal single-criterion ratings; categorical multi-way tasks (ChaosNLI) have no mean and need a different decomposition (e.g., majority vs. minority mass).
