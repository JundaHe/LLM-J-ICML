The newly identified issues, ranked by severity, are:

1. **CRITICAL — The forecasting identity does not establish the advertised reliability interpretation.**

   §2.1 defines both residuals using the comparator computed from panel 1:

   > “\(r_1=C(h_1-q_T)\) and \(r_2=C(h_2-q_T)\), with \(q_T\) computed from \(P_1\).”

   That comparator preserves **panel 1’s** mode and mean. Consequently, \(r_2\) is not panel 2’s shape after removing its own location. Writing \(q_j=q_T(P_j)\),

   \[
   r_2=C(h_2-q_2)+C(q_2-q_1).
   \]

   Thus X contains an additional cross-term involving the difference between panel locations and comparators. The paper must show why that term does not compromise the intended interpretation.

   The algebraic forecasting identity alone does not prove that “\(M-X\) is sampling noise,” that ρ is reliability of shape alone, or that G/X is a fraction of reproducible shape. These are central scientific claims, not interchangeable names for the forecasting statistic. Either supply the necessary argument and calibration, or describe ρ more narrowly as an operational forecasting quantity.

2. **HIGH — The decoded object is not shown to be a valid distribution preserving location; the score equations also need normalization.**

   §2.2 interprets

   > “\(q_T+C^{-1}\hat r\)”

   as a forecast differing only in shape. An unconstrained ridge prediction need not produce nonnegative probabilities, a monotone CDF, or the original modal class. Preserving location in the training residuals does not establish every required constraint on predicted distributions.

   Report constraint violations and the score after projection onto the recipient’s exact mode-and-mean cell.

   Separately, RPS includes division by \(K-1\), but M, X and G use ordinary-looking squared norms without that factor. With ordinary Euclidean norms, the displayed ordinal identity is \(A1=(M-2X)/(K-1)\). Define a score-normalized inner product or correct the equations and verify the implementation. This may be a notation fix, but “exact identity” requires exact notation.

3. **HIGH — The null findings lack a control demonstrating power to recover shape at the observed signal scale.**

   The positive control predicts:

   > “the panel-1 mean.”

   That checks alignment and location prediction. It does not establish sensitivity to a much smaller mode-and-mean-preserving signal.

   Likewise, the audit’s small total-RPS differences do not establish:

   > “Learned judges do not use the shape.”

   The relevant question is whether those intervals exclude a meaningful fraction of the **available shape gain**, using matched panels, rater counts and scoring units. Absolute RPS bounds can look small while remaining wide relative to the residual signal.

   A CI for one trained ridge procedure’s gain also does not bound all information available in the representation or text. The careful limitations acknowledge this; the findings and §4 repeatedly forget it. A planted shape control, calibrated to the observed noise and signal magnitude, is missing.

4. **HIGH — The proposed rater explanation has not explained the reproducible shape.**

   The strongest admission is:

   > “every model reproduces” X ≈ 0,

   followed by:

   > “we have not tested whether the threshold model reproduces it at that size.”

   Reproducing an approximately zero statistic with every model does not distinguish the threshold explanation. Where dense-panel reliability is clearly positive—40 raters—the decisive model check is absent. Where SummEval has a residual, the models fail:

   > “neither thresholds nor one-dimensional dispersion reproduce” it.

   Moreover, M1’s likelihood gain can reflect rater severity or scale use without explaining the histogram residual conditional on exact mode and mean. Random-cell prediction with known rater identities answers a different question from text-conditioned forecasting for new raters.

   **Required experiment:** simulate M0, M1 and M2 at the same 40-rater splits used for the observed positive reliability; compare their predicted M, X and ρ against the observations, with uncertainty. The mechanism claim should depend on M1 explaining something M0 cannot.

5. **HIGH — The budget explanation contradicts the introduction, and “always” contradicts the limitations.**

   §1 says:

   > “we therefore do not derive the budget result from the mechanism.”

   §5 nevertheless claims:

   > “the explanation of §§2–4 for why the raters’ side of the budget buys so little.”

   These are incompatible positions. The registered prediction’s failure is a reason to keep mechanism and budget evidence separate.

   The rule also says:

   > “on large item sets frozen-feature heads always … preferred more items.”

   But §7 reports HelpSteer2 on OLMo-2, where:

   > “1436 × 2 edges out 2870 × 1 by 0.0007.”

   That exception is small, but it directly falsifies “always.” POPQUORN’s substantial benefit from a second rating further limits the claim that the rater side “buys so little.” The supported conclusion is conditional on task, learner and design.

6. **HIGH — The uncertainty claims do not consistently match the population being discussed.**

   The paper promises reproducibility across raters and forecasting a “new rater,” but generally specifies:

   > “item-cluster bootstraps.”

   For panels where raters recur across items, those intervals do not automatically capture uncertainty from sampling the rater population. The POPQUORN position-versus-user intervals demonstrate that clustering choice materially affects the conclusions.

   Clarify which results condition on the observed rater pool, and which generalize to new raters. Report appropriate crossed item/rater uncertainty where identities exist.

   The pooling statement is also unresolved:

   > “inverse-variance pooled; panels are not independent.”

   Acknowledging dependence does not account for its covariance. Explain how the pooled intervals accommodate shared items/raters, or present the pooling descriptively.

7. **MEDIUM–HIGH — “Fixed budget” is approximate, and optimization exposure is unspecified.**

   Two advertised comparisons do not use identical rating counts:

   - SummEval: \(960\times1\) versus \(144\times7\).
   - DICES-990: the stated budget is 594, but one condition is \(40\times15\).

   These are arithmetic discrepancies, not invented experimental results. The SummEval discrepancy favours the more-rater condition, so it does not explain away the observed items-first advantage. Nevertheless, call the budgets approximate and state the rounding rule.

   More consequentially, “same batch” does not establish comparable optimization. Report optimizer steps, token exposure, stopping rules and tuning effort for each condition. Fixed epochs can give larger item sets more updates; fixed steps can give smaller sets much more repetition. Without this information, annotation allocation and training procedure are entangled.

8. **MEDIUM — The structural-zero claims require scale and tie definitions.**

   §6 states:

   > “with three raters the mode-and-mean cell determines the histogram.”

   §2 gives the narrower example:

   > “two or three ratings on a three-level scale.”

   Generalization across scales depends on K and how tied modes are encoded. “Exact mode” is undefined for ties.

   Also distinguish cells over **empirical histograms with the specified rater count** from cells over continuous probability vectors. A unique empirical histogram does not automatically force a KL-projected continuous comparator to equal it.

   Enumerate the feasible histograms under the actual tie rule and scale for each claimed zero-support dataset. The broader statement that “three to five raters … fix its location and nothing else” should be removed: it converts a combinatorial or low-reliability result into an absolute informational claim.

9. **MEDIUM — The nominal positive control changes the scoring rule.**

   §2 uses Brier for nominal tasks, but the audit contrast is:

   > “raises test KL by 0.016–0.052,”

   whereas the ordinal audit uses RPS.

   KL and RPS respond differently to changes in probability mass. The NLI intervention therefore does not by itself demonstrate that the ordinal audit would detect a comparable amount of learnable shape.

   Report the nominal intervention under Brier as well, normalize against the available residual signal, and show a control with comparable intervention magnitude and statistical power. This requires rescoring existing predictions if they were retained, rather than necessarily retraining models.

10. **MEDIUM — Essential definitions and verification results remain missing.**

   Undefined or insufficiently specified terms include:

   > “\(A1^x\),” “mode prototype,” “reliability-shrunk … locations,” “thirty configurations,” and “CSC.”

   The reader also cannot reconstruct which datasets/panels enter each experiment. Fourteen datasets, eight small-panel reliabilities, eight informative decoding panels, ten audit panels and eleven target-construction panels are not necessarily inconsistent, but require an explicit inventory.

   Report actual permutation and positive-control results, the fraction of items that can be permuted within exact cells, and whether greedy layer selection occurs entirely inside the training folds. Replace:

   > “−0.0000 [−0.0000, +0.0000]”

   with sufficient precision to convey the result.

The three most valuable changes would be:

1. **Text-only: narrow the claims and make the evidence reviewable.** Replace “unlearnable,” “do not use,” “matches,” and “always” with conclusions actually supported by the procedures and intervals. Remove the mechanistic budget explanation. Qualify the abstract’s bound by clustering method. Include the tables, appendix, bibliography and panel inventory; define ties, score normalization and all comparator variants. These changes require no additional model training.

2. **New experiment: calibrate the entire shape-recovery pipeline.** Using the observed scales and rater counts, construct both a zero-shape control and a known text-predictable shape signal that preserves mode and mean. Run the complete comparator, cross-fitting, permutation, model-selection and bootstrap procedure. Establish what X, ρ and G/X recover and whether their intervals cover the known quantities. Then compare ridge against a nonlinear or fine-tuned predictor on SummEval, POPQUORN and a dense panel with positive X, using valid distribution forecasts. This directly tests whether the negative result reflects absent signal or inadequate detection.

3. **New experiment: repeat the budget comparison with optimization controlled and a declared decision criterion.** Use the existing one-, two- and seven-rating designs on SummEval and POPQUORN, across Qwen and OLMo, with matched rating budgets, documented training exposure, common validation rules and independent rating assignments. Define a substantively justified noninferiority/equivalence margin before examining the new results; use document/user clustering and include training variation. Until then, “no difference detected” is defensible, while “matches” and a general annotation rule are not.
