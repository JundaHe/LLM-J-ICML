## A. Bridge interpretation

T8 supports a narrower conclusion than the current wording.

It establishes that, on commit split 0 and the observed LORO folds, aligning the data partition and reconstructing the same donor-generation procedure does not make the two pipelines agree. The frozen heads consistently show a positive mode-only penalty, whereas the single-seed LoRA estimates are heterogeneous around zero. Thus the estimand is pipeline-dependent.

It does **not** establish:

- That representation updating causes the difference.
- That the adversarial objective causes the difference.
- That optimizer variance explains the difference. Fold variation also contains evaluator and target-panel heterogeneity; it is not an optimizer-variance estimate.
- That split or permutation sensitivity is excluded generally. Only one split and one permutation seed were bridged.
- That the 0.4–0.6 hard-contrast ratio measures relative “sensitivity to target information.” The two contrasts use different losses and baselines: plain-KL hard–soft versus CE hard–Chen-with-adversary.
- That the twelve signs are twelve independent replications. Criteria share items and folds share the test partition.
- Strictly, that the donor assignments were certificate-verified item by item. [bridge_commit.py](/mnt/hdd2/jundahe/LLM-J/paper-2/phase2/bridge_commit.py) checks `n_changed` and mean TV, not a donor-map vector or hash. Say “deterministically reconstructed and matched the recorded aggregate certificate,” unless you add a mapping hash.

### Paragraph I would accept

> On the fixed commit split and six observed evaluator folds, we trained frozen-feature heads using the LoRA partition and a mode-preserving donor assignment reconstructed with the LoRA trainer’s deterministic matching procedure and random seed; its changed count and mean TV matched the recorded LoRA certificate in every fold. The frozen heads produced positive mode-only contrasts in all 12 criterion–fold cells, averaging +0.0038 RPS for What and +0.0090 for Why, whereas the single-optimizer-seed Qwen LoRA contrasts were heterogeneous in sign and averaged −0.0007 and +0.0005, respectively. Consequently, disagreement between the reported estimates is not explained by the pipelines having used different partitions or donor-generation procedures in this realized comparison. It remains attributable only to an unresolved bundle of differences—including the adversarial loss, representation updating, model/head capacity and training stochasticity—and does not identify a model-class or representation-updating effect.

Presenting heads as the primary **multi-panel instrument** is defensible because they provide breadth and repeated optimization. T8 itself does not validate heads as a proxy for LoRA; it demonstrates non-interchangeability. Therefore describe LoRA as an “end-to-end robustness check on two panels,” not a two-panel “confirmation.” Concordance and disagreement must both remain visible, and claims from the head panels must be scoped to frozen-feature learners.

### Highest-value additional arm

Add a no-adversary mode-permutation arm, `perm_mode0`, matched to the existing `chen0` objective:

\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{adv}}=\mathrm{perm\_mode}-\mathrm{chen},\qquad
\Delta_0=\mathrm{perm\_mode0}-\mathrm{chen0},
\]

and examine the interaction \(\Delta_{\mathrm{adv}}-\Delta_0\).

Run both no-adversary arms for the same optimizer seeds and folds, keeping the donor mapping fixed while varying optimizer seeds.

- If \(\Delta_0\) becomes heads-like while \(\Delta_{\mathrm{adv}}\) remains near zero, the adversarial term is implicated.
- If \(\Delta_0\) remains reproducibly near zero, the discrepancy survives removal of the adversary; representation updating, capacity and output architecture remain bundled.
- If either contrast changes sign across optimizer seeds within the same fold, optimizer stochasticity is directly demonstrated.

For closer loss parity with the heads, an additional sensitivity with \(\alpha=1\) would be useful. No single arm can uniquely identify “representation updating”; that ultimately requires a frozen-versus-updated comparison inside the same LM scoring architecture.

## B. Conditional LoRA intervals

T3 should use the following label:

> **95% hash-clustered test-item bootstrap interval conditional on the fitted fold-specific models, observed evaluator set, item split, optimizer seed and realized donor mapping. Arm-calibrated intervals additionally condition on the fitted calibration maps. These intervals do not include uncertainty from retraining, alternative splits, donor mappings, evaluator sampling or calibration fitting.**

Do not call them simply “95% CIs.” The fold-specific points or range should accompany the pooled conditional interval. A narrow item-bootstrap interval and sign-changing fold estimates are not contradictory: they concern different sources of variation.

The absolute minimum defensible optimizer design is:

- Three optimizer seeds.
- Both `chen` and `perm_mode` retrained within every seed–fold cell.
- The same fixed split and at least the same three evaluator folds.
- A fixed donor mapping if the goal is specifically to estimate optimizer variability.
- Seed-specific estimates plus inference that resamples optimizer seeds and test hashes; calibration must be refitted in each bootstrap draw.

A balanced \(3\times3\) seed–fold block supports a claim explicitly conditional on Qwen, split 0 and evaluator folds 0–2. A claim covering the full observed commit rater panel requires \(3\times6\). Three seeds are an absolute floor, not strong variance estimation; five would be preferable if the effect remains of order 0.001–0.005.

The current chain7 design changes optimizer and permutation seeds together. It can support robustness across three **joint optimizer/permutation realizations**, but it cannot attribute variability to the optimizer. Do not call those factors orthogonal.

## C. DICES sign instability

Report the three estimates together:

> The DICES mode-only contrast was small and sensitive to the realized training/permutation stream: −0.0015±0.0008 in the earlier run, +0.0022±0.0011 in the `_v6` run and +0.0039±0.0014 in the G×R run. The ± values are descriptive configuration SEMs and do not capture this between-stream variability. We therefore do not infer either a positive or negative DICES location effect.

Do not average the three post hoc, and do not select `_v6` merely because it agrees with the proposed explanation.

This materially affects T7. Preserve the prespecified G×R realization for transparency, but change the DICES “✓” to “nominally concordant in the prespecified stream; not RNG-robust,” or remove checkmarks entirely. The claimed held-out result becomes:

> The predicted sign occurred in 2/4 panels in the prespecified realization, but one of those two cases—DICES—changed sign across otherwise comparable RNG streams.

Moreover, HelpSteer2 cannot count as a successful “both approximately zero” prediction without a prespecified indifference region. The held-out table therefore provides no robust validation of G×R; it remains an exploratory diagnostic.

## Verdict

T8 is valuable: it converts an apparent implementation mismatch into a genuine, unresolved pipeline discrepancy. It supports a heads-primary presentation only if the claims are explicitly scoped to frozen-feature learners and LoRA is shown as a separate robustness study. The paper is still not draft-ready until the corrected seed blocks finish and the adversary-versus-updating ambiguity is either tested with `perm_mode0` or left explicitly unresolved.
