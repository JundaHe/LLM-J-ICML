- The near-zero result survives common-map and arm-specific calibration; the mode-only contrast remains positive under both.
- Important defect: POPQUORN provides persistent `user_id`s. The loader discards them and randomly converts ratings into positions. Of 262 retained annotators, 259 occur in all three item partitions; every test evaluator user in each audited fold also appears among training-target ratings. Thus this is leave-one-rating-out within each item, not leave-one-annotator-out. It is not input leakage—the judge never sees user identity—but it estimates performance against the same finite rater pool.
- Item resampling is correct for generalization over comments conditional on that realized annotation panel. It is insufficient for inference to new annotators because ratings from the same user are dependent across items. Describe the intervals as item-bootstrap intervals conditional on the finite rater panel, or add an item-by-user/multiway-cluster sensitivity.
- The frozen-analysis claim is supported: the analysis and chain were committed at 12:26, while the first output appeared at 12:47; the operational diff changes only the panel/root, grouping unit, labels, and output filename.
- ±.001 was fixed before POPQUORN outputs, but inherited from a margin chosen after seeing SummEval. Call it a “prospectively applied POPQUORN sensitivity threshold inherited from the post hoc SummEval analysis,” not a prespecified or substantively justified equivalence margin.

## 3. Exact manuscript text

**Contribution statement**

> We introduce a support- and dose-certified donor-replacement audit of distributional supervision. Across ten ordinal frozen-feature panels and two end-to-end LoRA datasets, the evaluated mode-and-mean-preserving replacements produced small average raw-RPS contrasts, whereas mode-only replacement produced positive contrasts in all nine observed seed-by-fold cells on both LoRA datasets; these findings remain conditional on the learners, splits, assignment rule, realized doses, and annotation panels studied.

**Abstract**

> Distributional supervision for learned judges is often interpreted as teaching how humans disagree. We audit the predictive consequences of replacing training histograms with donor-derived targets while retaining either their mode or their mode and mean, and report intervention support and total-variation dose. Across ten ordinal frozen-feature panels, exact mode-and-mean-preserving replacement changed average RPS by −0.0024 to +0.0006. In end-to-end Qwen2.5-7B LoRA blocks, its raw contrast was −0.0001 [−0.0011, +0.0011] on SummEval coherence and +0.0000 [−0.0009, +0.0009] on POPQUORN offensiveness. Mode-only replacement instead increased RPS by +0.0120 [+0.0086, +0.0155] and +0.0029 [+0.0009, +0.0050], respectively, with positive estimates in all nine observed cells on each dataset. Complementary ChaosNLI results showed positive residual-allocation penalties under a different nominal construction. These learner-, dose-, and panel-specific results do not establish that full disagreement distributions are generally unnecessary.

## 4. Verdict

No-go for a top-AI venue as currently packaged. The second LoRA dataset removes round 15’s dominant breadth objection, but the POPQUORN rater handling needs explicit conditional framing or a rater-aware sensitivity, and the central near-zero result still cannot distinguish unavailable stable residual information from learner failure.

A/G would supply that missing annotation-side result: whether residual shape beyond mode and mean predicts an independent annotation panel, how reliable that shape is under finite raters, and whether learned-judge effects track stable residual variance and dose. That would materially strengthen both novelty and interpretation rather than merely add another model ablation.
