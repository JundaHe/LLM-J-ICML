# C. Evidence-base adequacy

**Borderline yes for submission, provided the claims remain as narrow as the current estimand language; no for any broad claim about learned judges generally.**

The fallback evidence is now defensible because it has:

- ten ordinal head panels, including three strong coverage-plus-TV interventions;
- two substantive nominal replications with consistent direction, plus a mathematically appropriate binary zero-dose check;
- group-disjoint evaluation;
- a corrected positive control that succeeds on Buse–Weimer;
- a nine-cell, three-cluster end-to-end SummEval block; and
- unusually good intervention-dose disclosure.

The single most damaging remaining scientific weakness is still **only one end-to-end dataset, one base family, and one item split**. The breadth claim comes from frozen-feature heads, while the learned-judge claim has only SummEval. That leaves a straightforward reviewer objection: the multi-panel ordinal pattern may be characteristic of the frozen-head instrument rather than fine-tuned judges.

The real minimum cure is the proposed POPQUORN-offensiveness LoRA block with `chen/perm/perm_mode`, at least three joint optimizer–assignment realizations and crossed document/item inference. There is no genuinely equivalent GPU-free substitute.

The best GPU-free mitigation—without unfreezing the panel set—is one subset-wise v8 positive-control replication on a decisive high-dose panel such as POPQUORN offensiveness or CSC, using the existing features. That would strengthen the argument that the frozen-head assay can detect a learnable residual-shape signal in the same regime that supplies the main ordinal result, but it would not replace a second end-to-end dataset.
