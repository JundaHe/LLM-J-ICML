Overall: the score decomposition is mathematically exact, but the protocol currently overinterprets \(\Delta(a)\), misstates \(\hat N\), and has an incorrect binary negative control. I would fix those before running [FINITE_RATER_PROTOCOL.md](/mnt/hdd2/jundahe/LLM-J/paper-2/phase2/FINITE_RATER_PROTOCOL.md).

1. **Identity and estimators**

Given conditionally i.i.d. ratings from \(p_i\), with \(P2,P3\) independent of \(P1\), the identity is exact:

\[
A1(a)=N(a)-\Delta(a).
\]

There is no additional selection/covariance term from \(q_T\) depending on \(T(h_1)\): condition on \(P1\) and the other-fold training data, then each held-out histogram has expectation \(p_i\).

The cross-product estimator is therefore unbiased for the risk of the actual cross-fitted \(q_T\):

\[
E\langle C(h_2-q_T),C(h_3-q_T)\rangle
=\|C(p-q_T)\|^2.
\]

“Exchangeable” alone is insufficient; state conditional i.i.d. Persistent-rater analyses instead estimate a rater-pool-specific quantity and need the proposed rater sensitivity.

The displayed \(\hat N\) omits division by \(a\). Replace its description with:

> \[
> \widehat N_i(a)=\frac1n\sum_k
> \frac{\widehat F_{ik}(1-\widehat F_{ik})}{a-1},
> \]
> with \(\widehat F_{ik}\) replaced by \(\widehat p_{ik}\) for Brier. Equivalently, estimate \(F_k(1-F_k)\) by \(a\widehat F_k(1-\widehat F_k)/(a-1)\), then divide by \(a\). This is conditionally unbiased for both RPS and Brier.

2. **Meaning of \(\Delta\)**

\(\Delta(a)\) is not itself “residual information.” It is the latent risk of the chosen \(T\)-only forecast. It includes:

- irreducible variation beyond \(T\);
- noise in finite-panel \(T(h_1)\);
- estimation/smoothing error in \(q_T\);
- error introduced by forcing \(q_T\) to reproduce noisy empirical \(T\).

The operational residual advantage is \(\Delta-N=-A1\). Genuine conditional information is the Bayes-risk difference

\[
I(a)=R(E[p\mid T(h_1)])-R(E[p\mid h_1])\ge0,
\]

which this learner-free plug-in design does not estimate.

Replace “predictive value…” with:

> \(\Delta(a)\) is the score-metric error of the prospectively specified, cross-fitted \(T(h_1)\)-only comparator. Accordingly, \(-A1(a)=\Delta(a)-N(a)\) is the predictive advantage of the raw residual-bearing histogram over that comparator; it is not a general Bayes residual-information quantity.

Report a secondary \(\Delta_{\mathrm{ref}}\) using \(T\) estimated from an independent 40-rating reference panel. Call it “reference-panel,” not “oracle.” This separates noisy-\(T\) effects from stable shape variation.

The crossover remains useful empirically, but call it the **raw-histogram/T-comparator crossover**, not when residual shape becomes “worth learning.” The algebra is elementary; cross-panel estimation and validation are the non-trivial contribution.

3. **Construction of \(q_T\)**

The hard “three items or fallback” rule is discontinuous and unstable, especially as exact sums become sparse at large \(a\). Averaging \(h_1\) conditional on \(T(h_1)\) also learns sampling-selection artifacts.

Preferred replacement:

> Construct \(q_T\) from independent response ratings of other-fold items: compute their predictors \(T(P1)\), but use their \(P2\) histograms as responses. Smooth continuously within mode over sum/modal count, shrinking toward the mode-only prototype; choose the single shrinkage/bandwidth parameter by inner grouped cross-validation using the panel’s scoring rule. Do not require the primary predictive \(q_T\) to reproduce noisy empirical \(T\). Retain the KL-projected, exactly \(T\)-preserving comparator as a secondary audit-aligned analysis.

4. **Prediction test**

Fitting three high-\(a\) points and extrapolating to \(a=2\) is not a credible confirmatory test: mode errors and discreteness are not generally \(1/a\).

Replace with:

> Fit \(N(a)=v/a\) and \(\Delta(a)=\delta_\infty+c/a\), \(\delta_\infty,c\ge0\), on \(a=\{2,5,10,20,40\}\); prospectively predict \(\{3,8,15,30\}\). Treat lack of fit as evidence against this two-term approximation.

5. **A2**

Keep A2 as secondary. It is the bridge to the exact learned-judge intervention, not a second estimator of A1. Report intention-to-treat and changed-item effects, changed fraction, and TV; rerun matching inside every bootstrap replicate.

6. **Moderator**

Bhatia–Davis normalization measures endpoint dispersion, not general bimodality—especially for \(K=5\), where peaks at 1 and 3 score poorly. Either rename it “normalised endpoint dispersion” or replace it with:

> Ordinal: \(B_i=\max_{v-u\ge2}4h_{0iu}h_{0iv}\). Nominal: \(B_i=\max_{u<v}4h_{0iu}h_{0iv}\). These \([0,1]\) scores measure balanced mass on two separated ordinal levels or two nominal labels.

Standardize within panel before combining coefficients.

7. **Critical negative-control correction**

For binary nominal ratings, \(T\) determines \(h_1\), so an exactly \(T\)-preserving \(q_T=h_1\). Consequently:

\[
A1=0,\qquad \Delta=N,
\]

not \(\Delta=0\). Replace the existing paragraph accordingly. Also state that every bootstrap reruns folds, \(q_T\) fitting, projections, and donor matching; otherwise its intervals omit nuisance-estimation uncertainty.
