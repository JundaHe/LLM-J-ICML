**Verdict: weak reject; low accept likelihood in the current form.** The revisions establish a more defensible forecasting result and substantially improve the audit trail. But the central interpretation of decoding as recovering a “fraction of the reproducible shape” remains unresolved, and the revised projected “bounds” are not confidence bounds. R19-9 has a useful prospective design; its pending outcome cannot yet support equivalence language.

### 2. Were the frozen readings respected?

**Mostly yes at the level of the recorded results; less consistently in the manuscript’s conclusions.**

- **R19-1:** Correctly applies the failed cross-term-negligibility criterion and changes the operational interpretation to forecasting. The manuscript has not propagated that change everywhere.
- **R19-2:** Correctly identifies SummEval’s detection threshold and POPQUORN’s failure to detect a planted signal at the observed scale. The reported numerical readings agree with the table. Calling the observed recovery a “ceiling” goes beyond what this experiment establishes.
- **R19-3:** Correctly applies the simulation-range rule. The DICES/BW/Dorn distinctions are reported faithfully. “Reproduces” remains a descriptive model check using fitted parameters and twenty simulations, rather than identification of the generating mechanism.
- **R19-4:** The exposure audit answers the stated optimization concern. Nevertheless, its instruction to retain “not distinguishable” is violated by the manuscript’s continued “matches” language.
- **R19-5:** The reduction to \(R=5,B=100\) is explicitly disclosed as occurring after a smoke result. The final readings follow that amended design. Failure of the lower bound to exceed the ridge point means the declared improvement criterion was not met; it does **not** demonstrate that the nonlinear decoder is “no better.”
- **R19-6:** The numerical threshold reading is applied as written, but the rule itself overreaches. A crossed upper bound below \(0.5\) cannot validate the stronger population claim “\(\rho<0.3\).” Nor does an analysis at \(a=10\) establish uncertainty at \(a=40\).
- **R19-7:** The Brier readings and KL reproducibility check are applied faithfully.
- **R19-9:** The design clearly declares its margin with knowledge of prior results and applies it only to the new block. That is honest prospective confirmation of a selected hypothesis, conditional on this dataset and training setup. Three seed clusters provide limited support for bootstrap tail calibration; 2,000 bootstrap replicates do not increase the number of independent training realizations.

**R19-8a is transparently logged, but it is a substantive post-hoc amendment.** The original null criterion failed. The protocol explicitly labels the replacement “**new; not prespecified**,” which is good disclosure. However:

> “amendments … do not change the readings”

and

> “Readings (as frozen, applied to ΔG_proj)”

are misleading descriptions of what happened. Replacing \(G_{\rm proj}\) by \(G_{\rm proj}-G_{\rm proj,perm}\) changes the estimand, even if the numerical tolerances remain unchanged.

The correction is scientifically reasonable to investigate. It should be presented as an exploratory amendment following a failed original control, then validated on independent simulations. The manuscript currently cites R19-8 and explains the correction, but does not clearly disclose the failed criterion and post-hoc status.

### 3. New issues and newly exposed inconsistencies, ranked

**1. CRITICAL — The decoding interpretation inherits the cross-term problem that R19-1 diagnosed.**

The draft reports \(G/X\) as:

> “the fraction … of the reproducible shape”

But its evaluation residual remains \(r_2=C(h_2-q_1)\). Define
\[
s_2=C(h_2-q_2),\qquad d=C(q_2-q_1).
\]
Then the reported gain decomposes as
\[
G=
\underbrace{\frac{2\langle\hat r,s_2\rangle-\|\hat r\|^2}{n}}_{\text{gain against the other panel's own shape}}
+
\underbrace{\frac{2\langle\hat r,d\rangle}{n}}_{\text{location-comparator cross-term}}.
\]

Its denominator is likewise \(X_{\rm own}+X_{\rm cross}\), rather than \(X_{\rm own}\). Consequently, \(G/X\) is an operational normalized forecasting gain; it has not been established as the recovered fraction of shape-to-shape reproducibility.

Projection onto panel 1’s cell does not remove this issue. R19-1 already demonstrates that a residual preserving panel 1’s location can correlate with the other panel’s comparator shift. This problem affects the paper’s central interpretation, including the ordinal–NLI comparison.

**2. HIGH — The revised “bounds” combine different estimands and have no demonstrated coverage.**

The introduction defines them as:

> “the larger of the three-family 95 % upper bound and the cell-projected point estimate”

The first quantity bounds the unconstrained gain; the second estimates a projected, permutation-corrected contrast. Taking their maximum does not produce a confidence bound for the second quantity. Its sampling uncertainty remains unmeasured.

This matters especially where projection raises the reported value: SummEval relevance, DICES-350, DICES-990 and HelpSteer2. The abstract’s “**at most 10.3 %**” is therefore too strong. R19-8a itself acknowledges “**point estimates without an interval and computed on Qwen2.5 only**”; that qualification must accompany the headline.

Also, \(\Delta G_{\rm proj}\) is the performance difference between two projected forecasts. It is not the score gain over \(q_T\). Report those two comparisons separately.

**3. HIGH — The claimed rater population is internally inconsistent and incompletely covered.**

§2.1 classifies SummEval among panels whose columns are:

> “positions rather than persistent people”

Yet §4 explicitly includes:

> “the panels with persistent rater identities … the SummEval experts”

Scalabrino also appears in §4’s persistent-identity list but lacks a corresponding uncertainty treatment in §2.1. A dataset containing both identifiable experts and anonymous crowd positions needs a mixed population statement.

Moreover, the claim that an item bootstrap “**resamples them, so the intervals refer to new raters**” needs assumptions about independent annotation assignments across items. Anonymous positions do not establish independent people.

For DICES-990, crossed uncertainty is reported only at ten raters, while the headline discusses forty. That gap cannot be closed by the frozen threshold rule. Finally, §4 still calls dense-panel reproducibility “**small but non-zero**,” despite the newly reported crossed intervals including zero on BW and DICES-350.

**4. HIGH — The planted experiment is not a general power calibration or a “known linear shape” recovery ceiling.**

The abstract describes:

> “a known linear shape”

The generator makes **log dispersion** linear in features:
\[
\log \sigma_i=\text{constant}+\gamma s_i.
\]
Thresholding and subtraction of an empirical exact-cell comparator need not make the decoded residual linear in those features. Changing dispersion can also change observable mean and mode, even with latent location fixed.

Thus low recovery does not isolate a decoder bottleneck for a linearly representable residual. Nor can one random direction and one simulated matrix establish the statement that the pipeline “would have detected” arbitrary text-predictable shape of that magnitude.

The observed 7–12% recovery is a calibration result for this construction, not an upper ceiling. The same qualification applies to the projected 17–20%.

**5. MEDIUM — The zero-shape nominal sham produces an unexplained contrast.**

R19-7 says:

> “αNLI (K = 2): the modal mass fixes the histogram … pzc − soft is ±0.003”

When the transformation cannot change the histogram, any arm difference must come from another source, such as optimization randomness. Excluding this panel from the substantive claim is appropriate, but does not explain the sham result.

Verify target identity and repeat the sham with matched initialization, minibatch order and stopping rules, or explicitly quantify the unmatched optimization variation. This is relevant to interpreting the smaller nominal intervention costs.

**6. MEDIUM — The nonlinear limitation directly contradicts the new analysis.**

§7 states:

> “not with non-linear or fine-tuned probes”

But §2.2 and R19-5 report Gaussian-kernel random-feature probes. Replace this with the actual scope: one additional nonlinear probe, on two panels, with the stated computational amendment.

The neighboring statement that “**all frozen-feature results … replicate**” should likewise specify which panels and estimands were replicated. The projected contrast was evaluated only on Qwen2.5.

**7. MEDIUM to LOW — The inventory and numerical audit remain incomplete.**

Concrete items to reconcile:

- The abstract says **14 datasets** and seven SE datasets, while **MultiPref** additionally appears in §2.1 and R19-8a. Identify whether it is included, excluded or an alias.
- At forty raters, the introduction gives Dorn’s \(\rho_{\rm own}\) range as **0.12–0.22**; R19-1 lists **0.13, 0.13 and 0.22**. Reconcile the lower endpoint or identify the differing calculation.
- §2.1 prints the DICES-990 crossed upper bound as **0.09**; the protocol table reports **0.095**. An upper bound should retain sufficient precision or round outward.
- §5 still prints an endpoint as **−0.0000**, despite the text-correction instruction to increase precision.
- The draft remains dated **2026-10-27**, although it incorporates November analyses and a pending November block.

These are individually small, but inconsistent with the front matter’s assertion that all claims have been checked.

### 4. The three most valuable remaining changes

1. **Text-only: complete the narrowing and supply the missing definitions.**  
   Replace “matches,” “equally good,” “tie,” “never,” “mostly not in the text,” and unrestricted statements about what judges learn with the actual tested contrasts and populations. Disclose R19-8’s failed control and R19-8a’s post-hoc status. Include the panel inventory, scales, tie rule, exact comparator and shrinkage definitions. Distinguish structural impossibility from low empirical reproducibility.

2. **New decoding analysis and calibration: test the claimed shape estimand.**  
   On the headline decoding panels, compute each panel’s own comparator and report the decoded gain against the other panel’s own residual, its comparator-shift cross-term, and \(X_{\rm own}\). Separately report valid projected forecasting gains and permutation-corrected contrasts with paired uncertainty intervals. Repeat the planted controls with independent simulation draws and feature directions; if retaining “linear shape,” include a feasible residual plant linear in the decoder inputs within fixed location cells. Calibrate the final projected pipeline rather than treating its point estimates as bounds.

3. **Complete the already-running R19-9 unchanged.**  
   Report equivalence, non-inferiority and items-first outcomes separately under the declared readings. Retain the assignment and optimization details and the limited seed-cluster scope. No additional budget experiment is necessary for a narrowly stated SummEval conclusion; general budget guidance must remain conditional on the observed task and learner.

The paper has a credible narrower contribution: measuring the forecasting value of empirical histograms and auditing sensitivity to target construction. Acceptance currently depends on repairing the stronger claim about how much reproducible disagreement is available in text.
