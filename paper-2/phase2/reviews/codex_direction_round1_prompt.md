ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY: Soft-label / distributional fine-tuning of LLM judges (Chen et al., NeurIPS 2025, KL to the human rating histogram + adversarial term) is claimed to make judges "model human disagreement". We built an attribution audit: a fourth training arm whose per-item histograms are permuted across items under a label-preserving maximum matching (mode, mean and total preserved; only the item<->histogram-shape link is broken). On software-engineering panels (commit messages 800 items x 6 raters; code readability 124 items x 9 raters), with leave-one-rater-out identity hold-out and symmetric per-arm calibration, soft labels do help (Delta_soft = +0.0013..+0.0036 RPS, significant, two model families), but breaking the item link costs nothing (Delta_link ~ 0 in all six cells, MDE ~ 0.0005, permutation intensity .30-.55). So the gain is a global calibration/regularisation effect, not item-level disagreement learning. Our literature review then found Kohli et al. 2026 (arXiv 2605.29797) showing on ChaosNLI that KL-matching saturates at ~10 annotators while item-level entropy correlation needs 20-50, and that label smoothing already recovers much of the KL gain. So our null is explained and our "smoothing arm / panel-size curve" ideas are now replications. Every method in the review that shows item-level evidence feeds annotator identity, history or rationales into the INPUT; methods that only change the loss or output head do not.
