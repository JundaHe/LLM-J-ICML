# A. Sentence-level vetoes

## §1 Introduction

1. **Veto**

> “The usual justification is that a histogram of ratings carries information a single label discards: how strongly annotators agree, which neighbouring levels are plausible, and how ‘disagreement-prone’ an item is.”

“Disagreement-prone” treats noisy empirical dispersion as a latent item property.

**Replacement:** “A rating histogram records information discarded by a single aggregate label, including the observed concentration of ratings and which neighbouring levels received mass; whether these empirical differences generalize to new raters is an empirical question.”

2. **Veto unless supported with specific citations**

> “Distributional objectives (KL to the empirical histogram, often with an adversarial or smoothing component) do improve proper-scoring-rule performance against held-out raters on several benchmarks.”

**Replacement:** “Prior work has reported lower proper-scoring loss from distributional objectives on several benchmarks [citations], but those comparisons do not establish which properties of the training histograms drive the improvement.”

3. **Veto**

> “A histogram has a location (its mode and mean), a dispersion, and a residual allocation of mass across levels; a learner may benefit from any subset of these, and the ‘learns how people disagree’ reading implicitly credits the last.”

These are not disjoint components: after fixing mode and mean, the remainder can include dispersion, skew, and tail allocation.

**Replacement:** “Mode and mean summarize location; after fixing them, the remaining degrees of freedom include dispersion, skew, and allocation across the other levels. The broad claim that a model learns disagreement attributes predictive value to some part of that remainder.”

4. **Veto**

> “For each training item we replace its histogram by a donor histogram taken from another training item in the same cell, where the cell fixes the statistics we wish to preserve: the mode only, or the mode and the mean (with an exact KL projection when the donor's mean must be matched exactly).”

The mode-and-mean cell fixes a mean bin, not generally the exact mean; only `pmmx` restores the recipient’s exact mean by projection.

**Replacement:** “For each training item, we assign a donor histogram within either a mode cell or a mode-and-mean-bin cell. For the exact variant, we then KL-project the donor to the recipient’s exact mode and mean.”

5. **Veto**

> “Everything else about the training pipeline is held fixed, including the optimizer seed where we test it, so the contrast between training on original and donor histograms measures the average predictive value of the item-specific information that the replacement destroyed.”

This overstates both experimental control and identification.

**Replacement:** “Paired configurations use the same data partition and optimizer seed. Their contrast estimates the average predictive consequence of the realized target replacement, conditional on the learner, split, optimizer realization, assignment rule, and intervention dose; it does not identify the intrinsic value of the removed information.”

6. **Veto**

> “Because such replacements can only act on items whose cell contains more than one histogram class, we report for every intervention its support (the fraction of items that could be changed), its realised changed fraction, and its total-variation dose; these certificates turn out to be essential for interpreting null results…”

Support is an upper bound, and the heads results are not inferential “null results.”

**Replacement:** “Support is the fraction of items in cells containing at least two histogram classes and is an upper bound on the changed fraction. We report support, realized changed fraction, TV among changed items, and TV over all items because these quantities delimit the interpretation of small observed contrasts.”

7. **Veto for ambiguity**

> “LoRA-fine-tuned judges on two datasets … give a separate end-to-end study with Chen-style distributional training, an optimizer/assignment replication block, and a second base family.”

The replication block exists only for SummEval, while the second family exists only for commit Why.

**Replacement:** “LoRA-fine-tuned judges provide a separate end-to-end study: SummEval coherence has three joint optimizer–assignment realizations on one split, while the commit study consists mainly of single-realization configurations and includes a second base family on commit Why.”

8. **Veto**

> “The two settings are not interchangeable: a bridge experiment that aligns partitions and donor assignments between them exhibits a pipeline-level disagreement on one dataset, which we report rather than resolve.”

T8 matched the recorded aggregate donor certificate, not an itemwise hash, and only for one realized comparison.

**Replacement:** “On the realized commit bridge, aligning the partition and reconstructing a donor assignment with matching aggregate certificates did not eliminate the heads–LoRA discrepancy; the source of that discrepancy remains unresolved.”

9. **Veto**

> “At the highest-dose LoRA intervention (SummEval, 81 % of targets changed) the raw contrast was −0.0001 with a crossed-bootstrap interval of ±0.001…”

It is not the highest-dose LoRA intervention—mode-only changes 100%—and the interval is not accurately “±0.001.”

**Replacement:** “For the higher-dose mode-and-mean-preserving LoRA intervention on SummEval, 80.5–82.6% of targets changed across folds (mean 81.5%), with TV among changed targets of 0.335–0.351 (mean 0.344); the raw contrast was −0.0001 with a 95% crossed-bootstrap interval of [−0.0011, +0.0011].”

10. **Veto**

> “Mode-only replacement … increased RPS on SummEval in every seed and fold…”

**Replacement:** “Mode-only replacement produced a positive RPS contrast on SummEval in all nine observed seed-by-fold cells.”

11. **Veto**

> “On the nominal panel the residual allocation did matter…”

This converts a descriptive intervention contrast into a mechanism claim.

**Replacement:** “On nominal ChaosNLI, scrambling residual allocation while retaining the modal class and mass increased observed mean test KL at every tested annotation count.”

12. **Veto**

> “No constructed two-statistic target dominated the empirical histogram across panels.”

“Dominated” is undefined across metrics and panels.

**Replacement:** “No constructed target had the lowest observed RPS on every ordinal panel.”

13. **Veto**

> “What the learner uses depends on task and label geometry…”

One nominal dataset does not identify label geometry as the cause.

**Replacement:** “The observed effects varied across tasks and were consistent with a role for label geometry, but the ordinal–nominal comparison is descriptive and does not isolate geometry from dataset and pipeline differences.”

## §5b Discussion

1. **Veto**

> “On ordinal panels, preserving mode and mean left average RPS essentially unchanged wherever the replacement had support; disrupting item-specific location was costly on SummEval (both pipelines) and on several head panels, but not uniformly.”

“Essentially unchanged” suggests equivalence, “wherever … support” ignores realized dose, and “costly” overreads descriptive SEMs.

**Replacement:** “Across the ordinal heads panels, the observed exact mode-and-mean-preserving contrasts ranged from −0.0024 to +0.0003, with important coverage or TV limitations on several panels. On SummEval, mode-only replacement produced positive observed contrasts in both pipelines; positive head contrasts also appeared on several other panels, but the pattern was not uniform.”

2. **Veto**

> “On the nominal panel residual allocation mattered at every annotation count.”

**Replacement:** “On ChaosNLI, the residual-allocation scrambling contrast was positive in mean test KL at each tested annotation count.”

3. **Veto**

> “The simplest summary is that the value of distributional supervision depends on label geometry …”

**Replacement:** “The results vary across the tested tasks; the contrast between the ordinal panels and ChaosNLI motivates, but does not establish, a label-geometry explanation.”

4. **Veto**

> “When collecting ratings for judge training on 5-point ordinal scales with ≤ 5 raters, mode and mean already pin down most histograms, so the marginal value of releasing full histograms over (mode, mean) summaries is bounded by the support we report…”

This repeats the round-11 combinatorial overclaim. Intervention support does not bound the general value of releasing histograms.

**Replacement:** “In the tested three-to-five-rating panels, many observed mode-and-mean cells contained only one histogram class, which limited the reach of this donor-replacement intervention. This dataset-specific support result does not establish that mode and mean generally determine a histogram or bound the value of retaining full annotations.”

5. **Veto**

> “For nominal tasks the full allocation should be kept.”

One nominal benchmark cannot support a task-class-wide recommendation.

**Replacement:** “The ChaosNLI result cautions against discarding residual class allocation on nominal tasks without task-specific validation.”

6. **Veto**

> “Methodological contribution to carry forward: support/dose/TV certificates and three-estimand reporting for any supervision-ablation study…”

**Replacement:** “Methodologically, the study recommends reporting intervention support, changed fraction, TV dose, and clearly separated raw and calibrated estimands in supervision-ablation studies.”

## §6 Limitations

1. **Veto**

> “…the LoRA results [are scoped] to one base family (two on commit Why), one item split and — except for the SummEval block — one optimizer realisation.”

The item-split statement is false for commit Why.

**Replacement:** “The SummEval and commit What LoRA results each use one item split; commit Why uses three overlapping Qwen partitions, with a DeepSeek run on the original partition. Apart from the SummEval block, each reported configuration uses one optimizer realization and one donor assignment.”

2. **Veto**

> “On three-to-five-rating panels the mode-and-mean-preserving replacement changes 16–34 % of items…”

This mixes heads and criterion-level LoRA averages. Individual commit LoRA folds range from 25.4% to 37.1%.

**Replacement:** “In the heads experiments, the exact replacement changed 15.5% of commit What items, 22.6% of commit Why items, and 27.3% of HelpSteer2 items. Across the original commit LoRA folds, the range was 25.4–37.1% (criterion-level fold means 29.5% for What and 34.1% for Why).”

3. **Veto**

> “Only SummEval combines broad coverage with moderate TV.”

**Replacement:** “Among the tested ordinal interventions, SummEval provides the strongest combination of coverage and TV; Buse–Weimer and DICES have broad coverage but small perturbations.”

4. **Veto for insufficient labeling**

> “The SummEval 90 % interval inside post hoc bands is an exploratory sensitivity.”

**Replacement:** “For the primary raw SummEval contrast, the 90% interval [−0.00092, +0.00088] lies inside the post hoc ±0.001, ±0.002, and ±0.003 bands; this is an exploratory sensitivity, not a confirmatory equivalence result.”

5. **Veto**

> “…the SummEval block, whose three joint seed clusters give wide, cautious intervals.”

The key limitation is the number of clusters, not that the realized interval happens to be “wide.”

**Replacement:** “The SummEval crossed bootstrap resamples three joint optimizer–assignment clusters; with only three clusters, interval stability and generalization beyond the fixed split, folds, and family remain limited.”

6. **Veto**

> “Open: the adversarial term versus representation updating as the source of the heads/LoRA discrepancy…”

This falsely presents two identifiable alternatives.

**Replacement:** “Open: the commit discrepancy remains attributable to an unresolved bundle comprising the adversarial objective, representation updating, model/head capacity, output architecture, and training stochasticity. The fixed-donor no-adversary block was interrupted before it could distinguish even the adversarial contribution.”

The remaining quantitative statements in these sections—head range −0.0024 to +0.0003, SummEval raw and mode-only contrasts, ChaosNLI annotation-count direction, 52.8% original-grid boundary rate, and 3.0% extended-grid boundary rate—match the frozen results when properly labeled.

# C. §3/§4 claim audit

Beyond the numerical corrections above:

1. **“These are descriptive non-degradation results” remains too close to an equivalence reading.**

   Prefer: “These observed contrasts show no average degradation larger than +0.0003 in the evaluated configurations; the descriptive SEMs do not constitute equivalence tests.”

2. **Constructed-target language should consistently say “observed mean.”**

   Replace “hard was worse than soft on every panel but Buse–Weimer” with “hard had higher observed mean RPS than soft on six panels and lower observed mean RPS on Buse–Weimer.” Apply the same convention to the SORD comparisons.

3. **Mechanism over-read in the ChaosNLI paragraph**

> “the allocation of mass beyond the modal class carried predictive value…”

   Replace with: “scrambling mass beyond the modal class increased observed mean test KL under this learner and intervention.”

4. **“Mode-only replacement was partition-sensitive on Why” attributes the variation too narrowly.**

   Each partition has only one fitted optimizer realization and one realized assignment.

   Replace with: “The observed mode-only estimates differed across the three overlapping Why partitions; the design does not separate partition effects from realization-specific training and assignment effects.”

5. **The bridge conclusion needs the round-10 qualifier.**

> “The disagreement is therefore not explained by mismatched partitions or donor generation…”

   Replace with: “In this realized bridge comparison, the disagreement is not explained by the pipelines having used different partitions or donor-generation procedures.”

6. **Grid versions are currently blended.**

   Keep the λ≤0.5 result in the main T3b table and introduce the λ≤0.9 estimates explicitly as a sensitivity. Do not place the extended-grid interval beside a point estimate without identifying its analysis version.

7. **Positive-control wording is almost compliant.**

   Add “in the observed configurations” to “was learnable,” and retain the current restriction to the chosen feature-aligned injection. Do not generalize it to arbitrary residual shape.

The draft otherwise honors important earlier vetoes: it reports all three DICES streams, does not claim G×R validation, does not infer optimizer variability from folds, does not call LoRA a confirmation, and does not identify either representation updating or the adversarial objective as the bridge mechanism.

# D. Three largest submission risks

## 1. The central cross-pipeline discrepancy remains unresolved

T8 shows that heads and LoRA are not interchangeable on commit, while the fixed-donor `chen/perm_mode/chen0/perm_mode0` block is incomplete because of the hardware failure. The completed seed-2000 cells are insufficient for optimizer-level inference.

**Cheapest GPU-free mitigation:** Make SummEval the only main-text LoRA result. Present T8 as evidence of non-interchangeability, not mechanism, and move the commit LoRA table to an appendix as single-realization robustness evidence.

**If the block cannot finish:** Delete all suggestions that the adversarial term, representation updating, or optimizer variability explains the discrepancy. State explicitly that the planned fixed-donor block was interrupted after one complete seed and cannot support the interaction claim.

## 2. The headline still outruns the estimands

Heads uncertainty is descriptive; SummEval has only three joint clusters, one split, and one family; the class flow is deterministic; and the equivalence margins are post hoc. The design estimates consequences of particular replacements at realized doses, not what the learner intrinsically “uses,” nor a causal effect of ordinal versus nominal geometry.

**Cheapest GPU-free mitigation:** Reframe every headline around “predictive consequences of statistic-preserving replacement.” Keep raw RPS primary, move equivalence-band sensitivity to an exploratory paragraph or appendix, and remove general annotation-release recommendations. Expand the related-work and discussion stubs before submission.

## 3. The purported frozen evidence package is incomplete

The 250 files actually listed in `MANIFEST.json` all exist and match their recorded hashes and byte sizes. However, `manifest.py` uses `s0*` globs for commit artefacts, so the Why `s1` and `s2` analyses and cell predictions used in T3 are not in the manifest. Its recorded Git head (`c492fada…`) also differs from the current head (`db32bd1f…`).

**Cheapest GPU-free mitigation:** Explicitly enumerate every artefact used by every table, add Why s1/s2 and their analysis JSONs, regenerate T3 from those hashed inputs, regenerate the manifest and its checksum, and run an automated manuscript/table consistency check. No training is required.

# E. Suggested title and contribution statement

**Title:** *Auditing Distributional Supervision for Learned Judges with Statistic-Preserving Label Randomization*

**Contribution statement:** We introduce a support- and dose-certified donor-replacement audit that estimates the predictive consequences of removing item-specific histogram information while preserving selected statistics, revealing small average mode-and-mean-preserving contrasts on the tested ordinal panels, a positive SummEval mode-only penalty, and a contrasting residual-allocation penalty on ChaosNLI.

# Verdict

**Major revision / weak reject in its current form.** The core audit is interesting and the SummEval result is credible as scoped evidence, but the draft still contains several factual inconsistencies, residual mechanism language, an incomplete evidence manifest, and an unresolved commit discrepancy. Submission remains defensible without completing the GPU block only if commit LoRA is demoted to appendix evidence and the paper is framed as a dose-qualified intervention audit—not an equivalence, sufficiency, or label-geometry result.
